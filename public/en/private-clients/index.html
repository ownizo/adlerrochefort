Private Clients · Portugal

Private client insurance in Portugal: high-value homes, collections and the families who own them

A household with a main residence, a second home, a collection, a car or two and a family to protect rarely has one insurance question — it has several, usually bought at different times, from different insurers, without anyone looking at how they fit together. A contents section that caps any single item at a few thousand euros, a buildings sum insured that has drifted below rebuild cost, a motor policy that settles on a depreciated market value, a liability limit nobody has checked in years — each is a small gap on its own. Together, across a household with several residences or assets in more than one country, they are the pattern this page exists to review.

Where the risk genuinely needs it — a valuable collection, a non-standard property, a car worth more than a price guide shows — the answer is individually underwritten cover: an underwriter looks at the specific risk, agrees a value in advance, and writes the conditions into the contract before anything happens. Where it does not, a properly built retail policy does the job for less, and we say so. This page sets out how the review works, what underwriting requires, and where Portugal's geography changes the picture.

Households that also live in Spain are handled by the same adviser, to the same standard — and, where the insurer writes both locations, on the same policy. See private client cover in Spain.

What a private household typically needs to coordinate

Not every household needs every item on this list, and we do not assume otherwise. A coordinated review looks at which of these actually apply, and how they interact:

  • Primary residence, plus one or more second or holiday homes
  • High-value or non-standard homes, including renovation or construction works in progress
  • Art, collections, jewellery, watches, wine and other specialist collections
  • Collector or performance vehicles, and households with more than one car
  • Boats and yachts
  • Private or international health insurance, including for family members based in different countries
  • Personal and family liability, including exposure connected to a second home or staff
  • Domestic staff or a property manager, where a household employs one
  • Periods where a property stands unoccupied, and cover for possessions away from home

The point of reviewing these together, rather than policy by policy, is straightforward: a gap in one usually shows up as a gap in another, and nobody notices until a claim.

The problem, stated plainly

The single-article limit. A Portuguese multirriscos policy insures contents to a total sum, then caps what it will pay for any one item within that total. The cap is typically a few thousand euros, or a stated percentage of the contents sum with a per-item ceiling on top. A watch worth €60,000 inside a €150,000 contents figure is not insured for €60,000; it is insured for whatever the single-article limit says, and the rest of the contents sum is irrelevant to it.

The proportional rule. Where the sum insured is below the true value at risk, Portuguese wordings reduce every settlement in the same proportion, including partial ones. Declare €700,000 on a building that costs €1,100,000 to rebuild, suffer a €120,000 fire, and the arithmetic produces roughly €76,000. Above a certain rebuild value this stops being a technicality and starts being the whole outcome of a claim, because construction costs move and a sum insured set at purchase drifts every year it is not revisited.

Settlement on depreciated market value. A standard motor policy pays what the vehicle was worth on the day of the loss, calculated from a trade table. For a car that has appreciated — a restored example, a limited series, a car with more spent on it than a price guide will ever show — that figure is not the car. The same logic applies to a painting settled at what a comparable one fetched, or a piece of jewellery settled at scrap and stone value rather than what it would cost to have it made again.

The standard we place to

These are the reference conditions of the high-net-worth wordings we place for homes in Portugal. They are what we look for before recommending a policy, and what we explain line by line when a proposal falls short of them.

The house

On-site appraisal and rebuild cost

For higher-value homes the insurer carries out an on-site appraisal at no cost to you, to confirm the rebuild cost, advise on contents and valuables sums insured and recommend risk-management measures.

No proportional reduction

Once the recommended sums are accepted, the insurer waives average: a partial loss is paid in full, even if building costs have moved since.

Rebuild guaranteed

After a total loss the house is rebuilt even if the cost exceeds the buildings sum insured — provided the appraisal's recommended sums were accepted.

Comparable accommodation

Alternative accommodation to a comparable standard, including for pets and horses, for as long as the house cannot be lived in — not the few months typical of the retail market.

Grounds, walls and outbuildings

Trees, shrubs and lawns, boundary and retaining walls, pools, annexes and guest houses carried at their own sums insured — not capped at a token figure.

Water, gas and trace and access

Finding and repairing escapes of water, gas or heating oil with no separate sub-limit, and the water or oil that is lost.

Settlement your way

A cash settlement, or repair by the suppliers, craftsmen and restorers you choose, with no penalty either way.

Excess waived on large losses

Above a set claim size the excess is waived altogether — precisely where it would otherwise weigh most.

The modern house

Solar panels, batteries and backup generators, an environmental upgrade when rebuilding, and lock replacement when keys are lost or stolen.

Disability alterations

Adapting the home when a family member suffers a permanent disability, through injury or illness.

Contents

Worldwide all-risks

Personal possessions covered against all risks — at home, travelling and at a second home — with no separate sub-limit for what you carry.

Contents above the sum insured

Once the recommended sums are accepted, a claim can exceed the contents sum insured by a pre-agreed margin if the true value proves higher.

No sub-limits where it matters

Accidental damage and loss, theft from cellars and outbuildings, and garden furniture without the sub-limits that hollow out standard cover.

Guests' belongings and new purchases

Your guests' possessions are protected, and newly acquired items are covered automatically for a declaration period.

Events at home

Event cancellation and temporary structures — marquees, stages — for celebrations held at home.

Art, jewellery and collections

Agreed value

Art, jewellery, watches and collections scheduled at a value fixed at inception against a valuation — the figure paid after a total loss, with no argument about depreciation.

No excess

Valuables insured on an agreed or declared value carry no excess.

Loss in value after restoration

Where a piece is restored but loses market value, the difference is paid — and repairs carry no cost ceiling.

Protection against under-valuation

If a professionally valued piece proves worth more than its sum insured on the day of the loss, the policy pays beyond the agreed value within a set margin.

New acquisitions and cellars

New pieces covered automatically for a period, and wine and spirits collections with their own storage conditions.

Family liability

Exposure grows with the household: guests, domestic staff, a pool, a second home, children studying abroad. A standard liability limit rarely keeps pace — and the difference is paid from personal wealth.

Limits that match the household

Family liability at limits of several million euros, worldwide.

Defence costs on top

Legal defence costs are paid in addition to the limit, not eroded from it.

Portugal is not one property market

What a review actually needs to look at changes with where the property is:

  • Lisbon, Cascais and Sintra. High-value urban and coastal residences, from historic apartments to estates in the hills — where the condominium policy ends and yours begins is the question that comes up most often, followed by listed-building rebuild costs in Sintra. See also villas in Sintra and Cascais.
  • Comporta, Melides and Tróia. Architect-designed and non-standard properties, usually a secondary residence, where rebuild cost and construction complexity are typically higher than a standard valuation assumes. See Comporta and Melides.
  • Porto and the Douro. Townhouses in the historic centre, riverside quintas and wine estates where the house, the cellar and the land are one household risk.
  • The Algarve. Quinta do Lago, Vale do Lobo and Vilamoura for higher-value villas with collections and staff on site; the western and eastern Algarve for coastal and renovated traditional properties owned as second residences. See Quinta do Lago and Vale do Lobo.
  • Madeira. Hillside villas and quintas on a steep, wet island, where the insurer asks about drainage, retaining walls and slope before it asks about anything else. See home insurance in Madeira.

None of this changes what is legally required — it changes what a sensible review actually asks about.

How we work

No client names appear anywhere on this site or in any other channel. No photograph of an insured item is published, used in marketing, or shown to anyone other than the underwriters approached on your instructions. There are no testimonials from this part of the book.

Everything runs in writing — email or WhatsApp — so there is a dated record of what was declared, what was asked and what was answered. That record is what a claim turns on. One person handles the file from the first enquiry through renewal and, if it comes to it, the claim; you do not restart with somebody new.

This is an operating method rather than a promise. It exists because information about what somebody owns, where it is kept and how it is protected is itself a risk, and the fewer places it sits the better.

What the underwriting will require from you

None of this is a formality. An underwriter pricing a specific risk rather than a category needs to see it, and what you supply at inception is what the claim is later measured against.

  • Valuations for anything to be insured on an agreed value — normally no more than three to five years old for jewellery and watches, and from a valuer whose basis of valuation is stated on the document.
  • Invoices, auction records or provenance for items without a formal valuation, including restoration invoices where more has been spent than a price guide would show.
  • Photographs of the property inside and out, and of each scheduled item, including hallmarks, serial numbers, signatures, hull numbers and any distinguishing damage.
  • Security measures actually installed — safe make and cash/valuables rating, alarm grade and whether it is monitored, CCTV, shutters, gates, and who holds keys and codes.
  • A plan or survey for the property: built area by floor, construction type and year, finish quality, and any non-conventional structure. Larger risks may require an underwriter survey on site.
  • Claims history, stated in full. Specialist underwriters price around a declared claim; they decline, or avoid the policy, around an undeclared one.

Frequently asked questions

Will the proportional rule apply to my home?
Not under the wordings we look for. Once the sums recommended in the insurer’s appraisal are accepted, average is waived: a partial loss is paid in full, and after a total loss the house is rebuilt even if the cost exceeds the buildings sum insured. That condition is confirmed in writing before we recommend a policy.
Can a home in Portugal and a home in Spain sit on one policy?
Often, yes. High-net-worth wordings can schedule several residences, their contents and valuables under one policy with a single renewal date where the insurer writes each location. Where that is not possible, we align the policies so limits, dates and conditions leave no gap between them.
What is the single-article limit, and where do I find mine?
It is the maximum a policy will pay for any one item, and on a Portuguese contents section it is usually a fixed euro figure or a percentage of the contents sum — commonly a few thousand euros. It is stated in the condições particulares, often under objectos de valor or bens de valor especial. A ring worth €40,000 sitting under a €3,000 single-article limit is insured for €3,000, whatever the contents sum says.
What does agreed value actually change?
On an indemnity basis the insurer decides after the loss what the item was worth, applying depreciation and market movement. On an agreed-value basis the figure is fixed at inception against a valuation, and that is the figure paid after a total loss. The argument about worth happens before the loss, in writing, while the item still exists to be examined.
Do I need a valuation for everything?
No. Underwriters normally want a valuation for items above a stated threshold and for anything to be insured on an agreed-value basis. Below that, an invoice, an auction record or a clear photograph with a description is often enough. For jewellery and watches, valuations are typically expected to be no more than three to five years old.
Is my Portuguese home policy any use for items I take abroad?
Usually only within narrow limits. Retail wordings cover possessions temporarily away from the insured address for a capped number of days, in a capped amount, sometimes only within the EU. Worldwide all-risks cover on scheduled items is a separate feature and has to be arranged deliberately.
How long does individually underwritten cover take to put in place?
Days rather than minutes. A straightforward household and valuables risk with valuations to hand is normally quoted within a week. A boat, a car collection or a risk needing specialty capacity takes longer, because the underwriter is pricing that specific risk rather than applying a rating table. Interim cover can often be arranged while the file is completed.
Will you publish or share what I own?
No. We do not name clients, we do not publish photographs of insured items, and we do not use any of it in marketing. Information goes to the underwriters we approach on your instructions and nowhere else. Everything runs by email or WhatsApp, in writing, so there is a record of what was declared and when.
