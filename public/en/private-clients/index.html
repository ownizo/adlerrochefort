Collections & Valuables · Portugal

Cover for things that are difficult to replace

Some property does not fit a Portuguese off-the-shelf policy. A contents section caps any single item at a few thousand euros. A buildings sum insured set from a purchase price triggers the proportional rule. A motor policy settles on a depreciated market value. None of that is a defect in the product — it is what the product was built for.

The alternative is individually underwritten cover: an underwriter looks at the specific risk, agrees a value in advance, schedules the items that need scheduling, and writes the conditions into the contract before anything happens. It takes days rather than minutes, and it requires paperwork from you. This page sets out how it works and what it needs.

The problem, stated plainly

The single-article limit. A Portuguese multirriscos policy insures contents to a total sum, then caps what it will pay for any one item within that total. The cap is typically a few thousand euros, or a stated percentage of the contents sum with a per-item ceiling on top. A watch worth €60,000 inside a €150,000 contents figure is not insured for €60,000; it is insured for whatever the single-article limit says, and the rest of the contents sum is irrelevant to it.

The proportional rule. Where the sum insured is below the true value at risk, Portuguese wordings reduce every settlement in the same proportion, including partial ones. Declare €700,000 on a building that costs €1,100,000 to rebuild, suffer a €120,000 fire, and the arithmetic produces roughly €76,000. Above a certain rebuild value this stops being a technicality and starts being the whole outcome of a claim, because construction costs move and a sum insured set at purchase drifts every year it is not revisited.

Settlement on depreciated market value. A standard motor policy pays what the vehicle was worth on the day of the loss, calculated from a trade table. For a car that has appreciated — a restored example, a limited series, a car with more spent on it than a price guide will ever show — that figure is not the car. The same logic applies to a painting settled at what a comparable one fetched, or a piece of jewellery settled at scrap and stone value rather than what it would cost to have it made again.

How we work

No client names appear anywhere on this site or in any other channel. No photograph of an insured item is published, used in marketing, or shown to anyone other than the underwriters approached on your instructions. There are no testimonials from this part of the book.

Everything runs in writing — email or WhatsApp — so there is a dated record of what was declared, what was asked and what was answered. That record is what a claim turns on. One person handles the file from the first enquiry through renewal and, if it comes to it, the claim; you do not restart with somebody new.

This is an operating method rather than a promise. It exists because information about what somebody owns, where it is kept and how it is protected is itself a risk, and the fewer places it sits the better.

What the underwriting will require from you

None of this is a formality. An underwriter pricing a specific risk rather than a category needs to see it, and what you supply at inception is what the claim is later measured against.

  • Valuations for anything to be insured on an agreed value — normally no more than three to five years old for jewellery and watches, and from a valuer whose basis of valuation is stated on the document.
  • Invoices, auction records or provenance for items without a formal valuation, including restoration invoices where more has been spent than a price guide would show.
  • Photographs of the property inside and out, and of each scheduled item, including hallmarks, serial numbers, signatures, hull numbers and any distinguishing damage.
  • Security measures actually installed — safe make and cash/valuables rating, alarm grade and whether it is monitored, CCTV, shutters, gates, and who holds keys and codes.
  • A plan or survey for the property: built area by floor, construction type and year, finish quality, and any non-conventional structure. Larger risks may require an underwriter survey on site.
  • Claims history, stated in full. Specialist underwriters price around a declared claim; they decline, or avoid the policy, around an undeclared one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single-article limit, and where do I find mine?
It is the maximum a policy will pay for any one item, and on a Portuguese contents section it is usually a fixed euro figure or a percentage of the contents sum — commonly a few thousand euros. It is stated in the condições particulares, often under objectos de valor or bens de valor especial. A ring worth €40,000 sitting under a €3,000 single-article limit is insured for €3,000, whatever the contents sum says.
What does agreed value actually change?
On an indemnity basis the insurer decides after the loss what the item was worth, applying depreciation and market movement. On an agreed-value basis the figure is fixed at inception against a valuation, and that is the figure paid after a total loss. The argument about worth happens before the loss, in writing, while the item still exists to be examined.
Do I need a valuation for everything?
No. Underwriters normally want a valuation for items above a stated threshold and for anything to be insured on an agreed-value basis. Below that, an invoice, an auction record or a clear photograph with a description is often enough. For jewellery and watches, valuations are typically expected to be no more than three to five years old.
Is my Portuguese home policy any use for items I take abroad?
Usually only within narrow limits. Retail wordings cover possessions temporarily away from the insured address for a capped number of days, in a capped amount, sometimes only within the EU. Worldwide all-risks cover on scheduled items is a separate feature and has to be arranged deliberately.
How long does individually underwritten cover take to put in place?
Days rather than minutes. A straightforward household and valuables risk with valuations to hand is normally quoted within a week. A boat, a car collection or a risk needing specialty capacity takes longer, because the underwriter is pricing that specific risk rather than applying a rating table. Interim cover can often be arranged while the file is completed.
Will you publish or share what I own?
No. We do not name clients, we do not publish photographs of insured items, and we do not use any of it in marketing. Information goes to the underwriters we approach on your instructions and nowhere else. Everything runs by email or WhatsApp, in writing, so there is a record of what was declared and when.
