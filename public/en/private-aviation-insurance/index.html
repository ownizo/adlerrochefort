Insurance for private aircraft, helicopters and drones

Private aviation is insured in a market of its own, with its own wordings, its own regulation and a small number of specialist underwriters. The questions that decide a claim — who is the operator, who is flying, where the aircraft is based — are often settled carelessly when the aircraft is bought.

We arrange aviation insurance for owners of private aircraft, helicopters and drones based in Portugal and Spain. It is placed exclusively through specialist aviation markets, via a co-brokerage partner that works in that market every day — and we say so from the outset.

Who it is for

Owners and operators of aircraft used privately, and estates that use drones.

  • Portugal: aircraft and helicopters based at Cascais (Tires), Portimão or Madeira, and owners who fly between the Algarve, Lisbon and Spain
  • Spain: aircraft based at Madrid (Torrejón, Cuatro Vientos), Málaga, Ibiza, Palma or Barcelona
  • Owners of piston, turboprop and light jet aircraft, and of private helicopters
  • Owners using a management company or operator, and owners of fractional shares or leased aircraft
  • Estates, vineyards and villas using drones for surveying, security or photography

The risks, and what differs between Portugal and Spain

The losses are physical damage to the aircraft — in flight, taxiing or on the ground — liability to passengers and to people and property on the ground, injury to crew, and damage to aircraft in someone else’s hangar. EU Regulation (EC) No 785/2004 sets minimum insurance requirements for aircraft operators, including third-party liability by maximum take-off mass and passenger liability; many owners carry limits well above those minimums.

What a well-placed policy includes

Aviation policies are built around the aircraft, its use and the people who fly it.

Hull all risks

Physical loss or damage to the aircraft at an agreed value — in flight, taxiing and on the ground.

Hull war and allied perils

Confiscation, hijack, terrorism and malicious acts, normally as a separate section.

Third-party and passenger liability

A combined single limit for passengers and third parties, at least at the level required by EU regulation, and usually well above it.

Crew personal accident

Death and disability benefits for pilots and crew.

Hangarkeepers’ liability

Damage to other people’s aircraft in your care, for owners with hangars.

Owner and operator arrangements

Correct naming of owner, operator, management company and lessor, so each is protected and nothing is voided by who is flying.

Fractional and leased aircraft

Cover that matches the ownership structure and the obligations in the lease or fractional agreement.

Drones

Liability and hull cover for drones used on estates, with the operator registration and operating category checked.

What usually goes wrong

  • A pilot who is not on the policy. Aviation policies list approved pilots or define pilot qualifications. A flight by someone outside that definition may not be covered.
  • The wrong named insured. Aircraft owned by a company and operated by a management firm need both named correctly. A mismatch surfaces only after a loss.
  • Commercial use on a private policy. Carrying passengers for reward on a private policy can void cover. Use must match the declared category.
  • Drones assumed to fall under family liability. Personal liability policies often exclude aircraft of any kind, including drones.
  • Agreed value never revisited. Aircraft values move with the market and with upgrades; an outdated agreed value leaves the owner under-insured.

What we need from you to obtain terms

Aviation underwriters work from precise technical information.

  • Aircraft type, year, registration, seats and maximum take-off mass, and proposed agreed value
  • Owner, operator and any management company or lessor
  • Pilots: licences, ratings, total hours and hours on type
  • Base airfield, hangarage, and the geographical area of operation
  • Use: private only, or any business or commercial use
  • For drones: model, weight, operating category and operator registration
  • Claims history and current policy

Frequently asked questions

Is aircraft insurance compulsory in Portugal and Spain?
Yes. EU Regulation (EC) No 785/2004 applies directly in both countries and requires aircraft operators to hold minimum third-party and passenger liability insurance. Hull cover is not compulsory by law but is normally required by financiers and lessors.
Who should be named on the policy — the owner or the operator?
Usually both, with their respective interests. Where a company owns the aircraft and a management company operates it, the policy must reflect that structure, or a claim can be disputed.
Do I need insurance for a drone used on my estate?
Very often yes. EU drone rules require operator registration for most drones, and national rules in Portugal and Spain require liability insurance for many drone operations. Family liability policies frequently exclude aircraft, including drones.
Why do you use a co-brokerage partner for aviation?
Because aviation is a specialist market with a small number of underwriters and its own practice. Working with a partner that places aviation every day gets better terms and a better claims service. We remain your adviser throughout.
What happens if someone not named on the policy flies the aircraft?
Cover may not apply. Aviation policies either name approved pilots or define minimum qualifications. Any change of pilot should be notified before the flight.
