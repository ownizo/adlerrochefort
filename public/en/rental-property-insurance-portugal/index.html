Rental property insurance for long-term lets in Portugal

A tenant on a residential lease is the most stable thing that can happen to an investment property, and the least visible. You are not there, and the first you hear of a problem is usually a message from an agent. This is what to insure, what the tenant insures, and where the two are assumed to meet and do not.

Where the owner stops and the tenant starts

A long-term let in Portugal (arrendamento habitacional) divides responsibility between two parties. Almost everything that goes wrong at claim stage comes from one of them assuming the other had it covered.

You insure

  • The structure: walls, roof, floors, windows.
  • Fitted kitchens, bathrooms, built-in wardrobes.
  • Installations: plumbing, wiring, heating, air conditioning.
  • Appliances and furniture you supplied.
  • Your liability as owner of the building.

The tenant insures

  • Their own furniture and belongings.
  • Their own liability towards you and towards neighbours.
  • Anything they installed or brought with them.
  • Frequently required by the lease — and frequently never actually arranged.

Nobody insures

  • Wear and tear on ageing installations.
  • Damage a tenant causes deliberately, in most wordings.
  • Unpaid rent — that is a legal matter, not an insured peril.
  • Improvements neither party declared.

Requiring the tenant to hold contents and liability cover is a lease clause worth including, and worth checking annually. Insurance you never asked to see is insurance that may not exist.

What to insure on a let property

  • Buildings at rebuild value. The cost of putting the property back, not what you paid for it. Construction costs in Portugal have risen sharply; a figure set at purchase is often well short by now.
  • Landlord’s fixtures and contents. The oven, the washing machine, the fitted units, the furniture in a furnished let. Insured as your property, because it is.
  • Owner’s liability. Claims arising from the building itself — a defective installation, a falling element, water reaching a neighbour. The detail is here.
  • Water damage. The commonest claim on a Portuguese rental, and the one that most often involves a third party downstairs.
  • Fire, storm, impact and theft. The standard perils, underwritten on a let property rather than an owner-occupied one.
  • Seismic cover. Optional in Portugal and often missing. Check whether the policy has it.
  • Loss of rent. Where available, income lost while an insured event makes the property uninhabitable. Not a remedy for a tenant who stops paying.
  • Glass and sanitaryware. Small covers that turn up disproportionately often in tenanted properties.

Everything on this page is general guidance. Sub-limits, excesses, occupancy conditions and exclusions differ between insurers and between policies, and the wording of your own policy is what decides a claim.

The gap between tenancies

A tenant leaves at the end of October. The next one signs in February. For those months the property is unoccupied, and most Portuguese policies say something specific about unoccupied periods — usually with conditions attached and some covers, escape of water in particular, restricted or suspended.

  • There is no universal threshold. Each insurer sets its own definition of unoccupancy in its own wording. A vacancy that is fine on one policy may trigger conditions on another.
  • Tell us before, not after. A vacancy declared in advance is an administrative matter. The same vacancy discovered during a claim is an argument.
  • Shut off the water. Between tenants, in an empty flat, over a Portuguese winter. It is the cheapest loss prevention there is.
  • Keep it looking lived-in. Clear the post, keep the garden cut, keep the shutters moving.
  • Longer vacancies need their own cover. If the gap will run for months rather than weeks, see unoccupied property insurance.

What is not an insurance problem

Landlords arriving from the UK, Ireland or the US often expect insurance to solve things that, in Portugal, sit with the lease and the courts. Being clear about the boundary is more useful than being encouraging about it.

Unpaid rent

Not an insured peril under a standard landlord policy. Deposits, guarantors and lease terms are the usual protections; ask us what, if anything, is obtainable for your case.

Recovering possession

A legal process with its own timetable. Insurance does not accelerate it.

Tenant disputes

A matter for your lawyer. Legal expenses cover exists in Portugal but is not a routine landlord bolt-on — see the honest answer on the landlord page.

Deliberate tenant damage

Commonly excluded. The deposit is the first line, and thorough inventories at check-in and check-out are the second.

Gradual deterioration

A boiler at the end of its life is a maintenance cost. Policies respond to sudden, accidental events.

Undeclared letting

If the policy still says owner-occupied, the cover is built on a fact that is no longer true.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between landlord insurance and rental property insurance?

In practice they describe the same cover. We use this page for the specifics of long-term residential letting — the lease, the tenant, the fixtures and the vacancy — while the main landlord page covers the wider picture including holiday letting and company ownership.

Does the tenant need their own insurance?

Your policy will not cover their belongings or their personal liability, so it is in their interest. Many Portuguese leases require the tenant to hold cover, and it is worth asking to see the certificate rather than assuming the clause did the work.

Am I covered if a tenant damages the property?

Accidental damage may be covered depending on the policy; deliberate damage by a tenant is commonly excluded. The deposit and a thorough inventory at check-in and check-out are the practical protections, and both matter more than most landlords expect.

What happens to the policy between tenants?

The property becomes unoccupied and most policies attach conditions to that. Insurers define unoccupancy differently, with no common national threshold, so tell us when a vacancy is likely to run long and we will confirm what your wording requires.

Can I claim for lost rent?

Loss of rent cover, where the policy includes it, responds when an insured event makes the property uninhabitable. It does not respond to a tenant who stops paying, which is a legal matter rather than an insured peril.

Is a let apartment different from a let house?

Yes, because the condominium policy covers the common parts and yours covers your fraction. Water damage claims in a let apartment routinely involve three parties, which is why liability limits deserve a closer look than usual.

I live abroad. Can I still arrange this?

Yes, and most of our landlord clients do. You will need a Portuguese tax number; everything else runs by email in English, including the claim if one happens.

Do I need a different policy if I switch to holiday letting?

Almost certainly. Short-stay paying guests fall under the Alojamento Local regime, which is a materially different risk. Tell us before you switch rather than after the first booking.
