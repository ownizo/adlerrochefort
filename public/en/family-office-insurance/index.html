Family office insurance in Portugal and Spain

A family office exists to hold a family’s wealth together across generations, companies and countries. Its insurance usually does the opposite: a D&O policy bought for one company, a home policy for each house, a yacht insured where it was bought, a life policy arranged years ago by a private bank — none of them written with the others in mind.

We work with single-family offices and the families behind them in Portugal and Spain to see the whole picture first — people, entities and assets — and then to place each risk where it is underwritten properly, in writing, with one adviser from the first review to any claim.

What a family office is — and why the risk is different

A single-family office manages the investments, companies, properties and affairs of one family; a multi-family office does the same for several families as a regulated or professional business. In Portugal and Spain the structure is often a holding company — a Portuguese sociedade gestora de participações sociais or a Spanish sociedad holding — sometimes sitting alongside a foundation, or alongside trusts governed by a foreign law, with family members, non-family executives and household staff spread across Lisbon, Cascais, the Algarve, Madrid, Marbella or the Balearics.

That shape changes what can go wrong. Decisions are taken by a small group of people who are both owners and managers, often on behalf of relatives. Money moves in large amounts between entities and countries. The assets are visible. And the people involved — principals, next-generation family members, trusted employees — are personally exposed in ways a conventional corporate insurance programme was never designed to answer.

Where the risk sits: people, entities and assets

  • The people. Directors of the holding and the family-office company, trustees and protectors, family members on investment committees, and the staff who execute payments. A claim against them is a claim against their own assets.
  • The entities. The holding company, its subsidiaries, the family-office company itself and any foundation. Each has its own governance, its own counterparties and its own exposure to regulators, creditors and minority family shareholders.
  • The assets. Main and second homes in two countries, art and collections, cars, a yacht, sometimes an aircraft or a rural estate — usually insured one by one, at different times, on different bases of value.

The review we do starts by mapping those three layers against the policies that exist today. It is the overlaps and the gaps between them, far more than any single policy, that cause problems.

What differs between Portugal and Spain

A family office that works in both countries deals with two legal systems. These are the points that most often affect the insurance; the legal and tax analysis itself belongs with the family’s lawyers and tax advisers.

How a family office programme comes together

1. A map, not a quote

We list the people, entities and assets, set them against the policies in force — limits, insured persons, territories, renewal dates — and send you in writing where the cover stops.

2. Priorities agreed with your advisers

We work alongside the family’s lawyers, tax advisers and wealth managers, so that the insurance follows the structure they have designed rather than the other way round.

3. Placement, line by line

Each risk goes to the insurers or specialist markets that write it properly, with proposals set out side by side: cover, limits, sub-limits, excesses and exclusions.

4. One adviser, every year

The same person reviews values and structures at each renewal and stays with any claim until it is settled. Information about the family goes only to the markets approached on your instructions.

Frequently asked questions

What does “family office insurance” actually mean?
It is not a single policy. It is a coordinated programme covering the people who run the family’s affairs (D&O, trustee and fiduciary liability), the entities they run (crime, cyber, professional liability where services are provided) and the family’s assets and lives (homes, collections, yachts, K&R, life cover). The value lies in designing them together, so that limits, insured persons and territories match.
Does a private holding company really need D&O cover?
Often, yes. Claims against directors of private and family companies come from shareholders — frequently relatives — from creditors, from tax and regulatory authorities and from buyers or sellers of businesses. Without cover, the defence costs alone fall on the director personally. Whether a policy is needed, and at what limit, depends on the structure and is set out in writing after a review.
Can homes in Portugal and Spain and a yacht be insured under one programme?
Frequently, homes and contents in both countries can sit under one high-value household programme, with agreed values and a single renewal, when the insurer underwrites each location. Yachts are usually insured under a separate marine policy, but the limits, liability and insured persons can be aligned so that nothing falls between the two.
Is life insurance taxed on death in Spain?
Generally, yes: when the beneficiary is not the policyholder, the sum paid is within the scope of the Spanish inheritance and gift tax (Ley 29/1987, art. 3.1.c), with rates and reliefs set largely by each autonomous community. In Portugal, sums paid under life insurance are outside stamp duty on gratuitous transfers. The tax position of a specific family should always be confirmed with a tax adviser.
Do you work with our lawyers, tax advisers and wealth managers?
Yes, and we prefer to. The insurance has to follow the structure the family’s advisers have built. We share our written review with them, on your instructions, and adapt the programme when the structure changes.
