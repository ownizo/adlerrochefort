Jewellery and watch insurance in Spain

A Spanish home policy pays a few thousand euros per piece. The watch, the engagement ring and the necklace that matter can be insured on their own policy, each piece listed with its value — without touching the insurance on your house.

Why the home policy is not enough

A Spanish home policy sets a sum for contents and then limits what it pays for jewellery and valuables. The limit per item is commonly between €1,500 and €5,000, and there is often an overall cap for all valuables together, of around 20 to 25% of the contents sum. Both limits apply at once, and the lower one wins.

Above that limit the piece is simply not insured, however high the contents sum. Standard policies also distinguish between robo — theft with force or violence — and hurto, theft without it: the watch that disappears from a bag or a hotel room. The first is paid; the second, above all away from home, is limited or excluded.

A policy of its own for what matters

  • All risksThe jewellery and watch policy we place in Spain is an all-risks policy for the jewellery and watches of private clients — beyond theft, it considers loss and accidental damage, on the terms of its wording.
  • IndependentIt does not depend on who insures your home. Your house policy stays where it is.
  • Each piece namedThe watch, the ring, the inherited necklace: listed one by one, with a description and a value.
  • For private clientsIt is written for individuals, not for jewellers or businesses.

Who it is for: residence in Spain

The policy is designed for private clients, Spanish or foreign, whose habitual residence is in Spain and who own one or more pieces of jewellery or watches. For British, American, German or Latin American families who live in Madrid, Marbella, Mallorca or Barcelona, that is exactly the fit.

If your habitual residence is elsewhere and the pieces only travel to a holiday home in Spain, tell us: the answer depends on where you live and where the pieces are kept, and we will say in writing which option applies.

What we need to prepare it

  • A list of the piecesDescription, maker, and for watches the reference and serial number.
  • Proof of valuePurchase invoices, recent valuations or gemmological certificates.
  • PhotographsOf each important piece.
  • Where they are keptSafe, alarm, and how often they travel with you.

Valuations age: a piece valued ten years ago is rarely worth the same today. If yours are old, we tell you which pieces to update first. For art and collections, see art, jewellery and watch insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to change my home insurance?

No. The jewellery and watch policy is independent of your home insurer.

I do not live in Spain. Can I insure my jewellery there?

This policy is designed for clients with habitual residence in Spain. If you live elsewhere, tell us where you live and where the pieces are kept, and we will say in writing which option applies.

What about theft away from home?

That is one of the main reasons to have a dedicated policy: a home policy usually limits or excludes theft without force away from home. What the dedicated policy pays is set by its wording, which we explain in writing before you commit.

My valuations are old. Is that a problem?

It can be. We ask for recent invoices or valuations for the most important pieces, so the values on the policy match today's market.
