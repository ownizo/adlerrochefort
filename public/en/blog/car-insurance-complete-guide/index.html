Legal requirements: what Portuguese law demands

Under Portuguese law (Decreto-Lei n.º 291/2007), every motor vehicle registered in Portugal must carry a minimum level of third-party liability insurance (seguro de responsabilidade civil). Driving without valid insurance is a serious offence that can result in fines ranging from €500 to €2,500, vehicle seizure, and personal liability for any damages caused.

The mandatory minimum covers bodily injury up to €6,450,000 and material damage up to €1,300,000 per claim. However, these figures represent the ceiling paid by the insurer — they do not protect your own vehicle, your passengers, or you as the driver.

"Mandatory insurance protects others from you. It does nothing to protect you from the financial consequences of an accident."

Pillar 1 — Cover types: from third-party liability to own-damage

The Portuguese market offers three main tiers of car insurance. Each one builds on the previous, adding layers of protection at an incremental cost. The single most important distinction is between cover for other people (third-party liability) and cover for your own car (own-damage, or danos próprios).

  • Third Party Only (Responsabilidade Civil) — the legal minimum. It covers damage you cause to other people and their property, but nothing that happens to your own vehicle or occupants. Typically suitable only for older cars with low market value.
  • Third Party Extended (Terceiros Completo) — adds important extras on top of the mandatory cover, such as theft, fire, natural events (storms, floods, hail), vandalism, broken glass, and roadside assistance. A sensible middle-ground for vehicles aged 3–8 years or with moderate market value.
  • Comprehensive / All Risks (Danos Próprios / Contra Todos os Riscos) — the most complete protection available. Includes everything in the extended tier plus own-damage cover for collisions, rollovers, and single-vehicle accidents that are your fault. Recommended for new vehicles, leased or financed cars, and high-value vehicles.

Own-damage cover (danos próprios) is where expats most often under- or over-insure. It repairs your car after an accident you caused, but it comes with a deductible (franquia) and is priced against the vehicle's declared value. On a car worth less than about €4,000–5,000, the annual own-damage premium plus deductible can approach the value of the car itself, at which point Third Party Extended is usually the smarter choice. On a financed or nearly new car, comprehensive cover is effectively non-negotiable — the lender will normally require it.

Within each tier, insurers offer optional add-ons that can significantly improve your protection:

  • Legal protection (protecção jurídica) — covers legal fees if you need to pursue or defend a claim in court. Litigation costs in Portugal can easily exceed €5,000.
  • Occupant cover (segurança de ocupantes) — extends injury protection to the driver and passengers in your vehicle, not just third parties.
  • Replacement vehicle — provides a temporary car while yours is being repaired after an insured event.
  • Roadside assistance (assistência em viagem) — breakdown cover in Portugal and often across Europe, including towing, on-site repairs, and accommodation if needed.

If you drive professionally — for example on a ride-hailing platform — standard private cover is not enough and can void a claim. In that case you need a dedicated commercial policy; see our guide to TVDE insurance in Portugal.

Pillar 2 — How to insure a car with foreign plates

Arriving in Portugal with a car on foreign registration is one of the most common expat situations — and one of the most misunderstood. The rules depend on how long the vehicle stays and whether you become a resident.

  • Short stays / valid foreign cover — if you are visiting and your car is insured in another EU/EEA country, that policy remains valid across the EU. A vehicle from outside the EU may require a Green Card or frontier insurance to circulate legally.
  • Becoming resident — once you establish residence in Portugal, you generally have a limited window to legalise the vehicle. The car must be imported and re-registered with Portuguese plates through the customs and IMT process, after which a Portuguese policy is required.
  • During the transition — while the import (legalização) is being completed, most insurers can arrange cover against the foreign registration or the pending Portuguese plate, so you are never driving uninsured. We routinely set this up so cover starts the day you need it.

Practical tip: keep the vehicle's foreign registration document, the DAV/customs declaration, and proof of the import request together. Insurers use them to identify the vehicle and to backdate your risk profile correctly. Pillar 3 — Transferring your no-claims bonus (bonus-malus)

Portugal uses a bonus-malus system: every claim-free year earns a discount on your premium (bónus), while an at-fault claim triggers a surcharge (malus). A long clean record can cut your premium by up to 50%, so protecting and transferring that history is one of the biggest levers an expat has over price.

The good news is that many Portuguese insurers recognise a no-claims record built up abroad — but only if you can prove it. To carry your discount across:

  • Request a no-claims / bonus-malus certificate from your previous insurer before you cancel the policy. It should state how many consecutive claim-free years you have and confirm no at-fault claims in the relevant period.
  • Get it in English or with a translation — a certificate on the insurer's letterhead, ideally covering the last five years, is what Portuguese underwriters want to see.
  • Present it at quotation, not after — the discount has to be built into the price from the start. This is exactly the kind of document we position correctly on your behalf so the saving is applied.

Not every insurer weights a foreign record the same way, which is another reason to compare several proposals rather than accept the first quote. Even a partial recognition of your history can be worth several hundred euros a year.

Pillar 4 — Average cost and the factors that set your price

As a rough 2026 guide, a Third Party Only policy for an experienced driver often falls in the €150–300 per year range, Third Party Extended around €300–500, and comprehensive cover frequently €500–900+ depending on the vehicle. These are indicative ranges only — your actual price is driven by the profile below. For worked examples and current figures, see our detailed breakdown of the average car insurance cost in Portugal.

  • Driver age and experience — drivers under 25 and the newly licensed pay significantly more; drivers over 30 with a clean record benefit from the lowest rates.
  • Claims history (bonus-malus) — your accumulated discount, including any transferred from abroad, is one of the single largest factors.
  • Vehicle type, age, and value — high-performance, newer, and expensive vehicles cost more to insure; engine size (cubic capacity) also matters.
  • Postcode and parking — Lisbon and Porto carry higher theft and traffic risk; a private garage can lower the premium.
  • Annual mileage — low-mileage and work-from-home drivers can qualify for discounts if they declare it.
  • Deductible (franquia) — a higher voluntary deductible lowers the annual premium. A fixed franquia (e.g. €300–500) is predictable; a percentage franquia grows with the claim, so read the fine print.

Several legitimate strategies reduce the cost without cutting meaningful protection: bundle multiple policies with one intermediary, pay annually instead of monthly (monthly plans add a 2–5% loading), keep a clean record, review the policy each year as the car depreciates, and fit approved anti-theft devices for a further discount.

"The cheapest policy is rarely the most economical. The true cost of inadequate insurance only becomes clear on the day you need to make a claim."

Pillar 5 — Documents expats need to get insured

You do not need to have completed every step of Portuguese bureaucracy to start a policy, but the more of the following you can provide, the faster and cheaper the process is:

  • Identification — passport or EU ID card, plus your Portuguese NIF (tax number). A NIF is the one item that makes issuing a policy far smoother; we can start a quote before you have one.
  • Driving licence — valid EU/EEA licences can be used directly; non-EU licences are valid for a limited period before they must be exchanged at the IMT. Keep the original and any IMT exchange paperwork to hand.
  • Vehicle documents — the registration certificate (Documento Único Automóvel for a Portuguese car, or the foreign registration document plus import/customs paperwork if it is still on foreign plates).
  • Proof of address — a Portuguese address determines your risk postcode; a rental contract or utility bill is usually enough.
  • No-claims certificate — from your previous insurer abroad, to transfer your bonus as described above.

Frequently asked questions

Does my no-claims history from abroad count?
Often yes. Many Portuguese insurers recognise a bonus-malus or no-claims certificate from your home country. Request the certificate before you cancel your old policy, ideally covering the last five years, and we present it at quotation so the discount is applied from the start.
Can I insure a car with foreign registration plates in Portugal?
Yes. While the car is being imported and re-registered, most insurers can arrange cover against the foreign plate or the pending Portuguese plate, so you are never driving uninsured. Once resident you must legalise the vehicle and take out a Portuguese policy.
What is the difference between third-party and own-damage cover?
Third-party liability (responsabilidade civil) is the legal minimum and pays for damage you cause to other people and their property. Own-damage cover (danos próprios) is part of comprehensive insurance and repairs your own car after an at-fault accident, subject to a deductible.
How much does car insurance cost in Portugal in 2026?
As an indicative guide, third-party only often costs €150–300 a year, third-party extended around €300–500, and comprehensive cover €500–900+ depending on the vehicle and driver profile. See our average cost breakdown for worked examples.
Which documents do expats need to get car insurance?
Ideally your passport or EU ID, a Portuguese NIF, a valid driving licence, the vehicle registration document (plus import paperwork if on foreign plates), proof of address, and a no-claims certificate. You can start a quote with just a passport and phone number and add the rest later.
Can I insure a car with a foreign driving licence?
Yes, while your licence is valid in Portugal. EU/EEA licences can be used directly; non-EU licences are valid for a limited period before they must be exchanged at the IMT. We explain the deadlines and arrange cover in English.
