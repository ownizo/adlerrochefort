Buying Property in Portugal: What Insurance Should You Consider?

A practical look at the insurance questions that come up when buying property in Portugal — not a conveyancing guide, a mortgage guide or a tax guide, just the cover side.

Buildings insurance, from completion

If you are borrowing to buy, your lender will require buildings cover in place from the point you take on the risk — typically at or before completion, not after you move in. Even a cash purchase should have buildings cover from completion: an uninsured empty property between completion and move-in is a real, avoidable exposure. See home insurance in Portugal for what this normally involves — rebuild value rather than purchase price is what the sum insured should reflect.

Condominium insurance, if you are buying an apartment

An apartment sits inside a condominium's own building policy, which covers the shared structure and common areas — the roof, the stairwell, the exterior walls. It does not cover your own contents, your own liability, or damage that starts inside your apartment and affects a neighbour. The two policies work alongside each other, not instead of one another. See condominium insurance for how the split between the two normally works, and ask to see the condominium's own policy before you complete — a poorly-insured building is a real risk you are buying into, not just an administrative detail.

Mortgage protection, if borrowing

A mortgage lender in Portugal commonly requests a life policy as security for the loan, and it is worth understanding early what your lender actually requires versus what it simply offers — you are generally entitled to use an independent policy that meets equivalent terms rather than the bank's own product by default. See mortgage life insurance for foreign buyers in Portugal for how this works in practice.

If you plan to let it out

A property bought with the intention of renting it — long-term or as a holiday let — generally needs landlord cover rather than standard home insurance from day one, because a standard homeowner policy is written around an owner living in the property. See landlord insurance in Portugal.

If your purchase touches more than one of these — a mortgage, a rental plan, a wider move — tell us once rather than filling in several separate forms.

A property above the range a retail policy is written for, or a purchase that adds to a household already holding more than one property, vehicle or type of cover, is often better handled as a coordinated review rather than a single transaction. See private clients.

Frequently asked questions

What insurance is legally required to buy property in Portugal?

If you are borrowing, your lender will require buildings insurance on the property, and life insurance is commonly requested (though not always legally mandated) as security for the loan. Beyond that, there is no single required property-purchase insurance — what else makes sense depends on the property and how you plan to use it.

Do I need insurance before I complete the purchase?

Buildings cover needs to be in place from the point you take on the risk, which for a mortgaged purchase is typically expected at or before completion. Confirm the exact timing with your lender or solicitor.

Is condominium insurance the same as home insurance?

No. The condominium's own policy covers the building's common structure and shared areas; it does not cover your apartment's contents or your own liability inside it. You need your own policy alongside the condominium's.
