Health Insurance Requirements for Portuguese Residence Visas: What Actually Qualifies

Published 10 August 2026 · 6 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

Every Portuguese residence route asks for evidence of health cover, and almost none of them says precisely what that evidence has to look like. The result is predictable: applicants buy a policy that is perfectly good insurance, submit a certificate that does not say what the caseworker needs it to say, and lose two weeks correcting paperwork with an appointment already booked.

This article sets out what consulates and AIMA are actually testing, what a usable certificate contains, how the requirement plays out on the four routes we are asked about most — D7, D8, Golden Visa and family reunification — and what changes once residence is granted and the public health service becomes available to you.

What the authorities are actually testing

Beneath the varying wording, the question is consistent: can you meet medical costs in Portugal, from the date you arrive, without relying on emergency public care? Everything a caseworker looks for follows from that. They are not assessing the quality of your policy or comparing insurers. They are checking that a document in the file evidences cover, for the right people, in the right territory, over the right dates.

Two consequences follow. First, the document matters as much as the cover — a strong policy with a vague certificate fails where an adequate policy with a precise certificate passes. Second, there is no single national checklist: requirements are applied by the consulate handling your visa or by AIMA at the residence stage, and they vary between posts and appointment types. Confirm the criteria for your own process before buying anything.

What a usable certificate has to show

Across routes, the documents that go through without comment tend to contain the same elements:

  • Every insured person by name, spelled as on the passport, including each dependant in the file.
  • Start and end dates that cover the application, the intended entry and the appointment, with the start date not later than the date you arrive.
  • Territorial scope that unambiguously includes Portugal, and ideally the Schengen area.
  • The insurer's name and a policy or certificate reference, so it reads as confirmation that cover is in force rather than as a quotation.
  • The benefits and any limit, stated plainly enough that a caseworker does not need you to explain them.
  • A language the caseworker reads — Portuguese or English; otherwise expect a translation to be requested.

What tends not to work: screenshots, payment receipts, quotations, insurer emails, and cover notes that expire before the appointment date. We go through the wording point by point in health insurance for the D7 visa: what counts as valid proof.

Route by route

D7 — passive income and retirement

The D7 is used by retirees and people living on pensions, rents or dividends, and it is the route where cover is most often needed before anything else is in place: no NIF, no Portuguese address, no bank account. That is a practical constraint on which policies can even be issued, and it is why many D7 applicants start with an international or expat-oriented policy and review it once they are resident. Age is the other factor: several domestic insurers restrict new applications above a given age, which narrows the options for exactly the group this route serves. Our note on health cover that still accepts you at 65+ covers that ground.

D8 — digital nomads and remote workers

D8 applicants are almost always arranging cover while living somewhere else, and often already hold a plan bought at home or provided by an employer. The real question is therefore not "what should I buy" but "can what I already have be evidenced for Portugal in the format the file needs". Sometimes it can. Often the certificate says nothing about Portugal, or the policy is written for residents of another country and quietly stops applying when your habitual residence changes — the clause that matters most and is read least. Because remote workers frequently move again, portability is worth more on this route than on any other.

Golden Visa

Golden Visa applicants usually have lawyers and fund managers handling the investment side, and what they need from an intermediary is a clean insurance document that does not delay the residence formalities. Cover is generally expected for the applicant and every dependant, valid in Portugal, with dates spanning the relevant period. Because these applicants often spend limited time in Portugal and continue to live elsewhere, a Portuguese network-based policy can be the wrong shape: it assumes habitual residence here. This is one of the clearer cases for international cover.

Family reunification

Family reunification is where certificates fail most often, for a simple reason: the file covers several people and the insurance document covers one. Spouse, children and, on some applications, dependent parents each need to appear by name. If family members arrive at different times, the dates have to work for each of them. Adding a person to an existing policy is normally straightforward — but it has to be done before the certificate is issued, not explained afterwards.

Where international cover helps, and where a local policy is enough

Both types of policy are accepted on the residence routes, and it is worth being clear that a Portuguese policy is frequently the better buy. It is cheaper, it gives you direct access to the private hospitals you will actually use, and consulates accept it.

International private medical insurance — IPMI — helps in three specific situations. It can normally be issued on your passport and current address, before you have a NIF or a Portuguese address, which is precisely the position most applicants are in. Its certificate is produced in English as standard, naming the insured persons, dates, insurer and benefits in a form a consulate can read without explanation. And it is portable, which matters if Portugal is one stage of a longer move or if you will continue to spend substantial time in another country.

Where it does not help is where the domestic product is already the better fit: a permanent move, treatment in Portugal, a premium that has to be sustainable for a decade. We set out the full comparison in Portuguese health insurance vs international health insurance, and the product itself on our international health insurance in Portugal page. Whichever route fits, cover and terms are subject to the insurer's underwriting — no policy can be promised in advance of it.

SNS access once residence is granted

Portugal's public health service, the Serviço Nacional de Saúde, is generally available once you are legally resident and registered. In practice you register at your local health centre with your residence permit, address and NIF, and receive an SNS user number. That is the point at which the reason for holding private cover changes: it stops being a document requirement and becomes a choice about speed and access.

Most residents keep private cover anyway — for consultations and diagnostics without a long wait, a choice of private hospital, and service in English while their Portuguese is developing. What private insurance does not do is remove you from the public system: emergencies in Portugal will usually run through the SNS regardless of what you hold. The trade-off is set out in SNS vs private insurance: do expats really need both?

One thing not to overlook: proof of cover can be requested again at residence-permit renewal. A policy that lapsed because the visa was granted is an avoidable problem at the next AIMA appointment.

Timing, and the failure points we see most

The pattern is almost always the same — the insurance is left until last, and there is no time to correct the document. A fortnight of margin removes most of the risk. The recurring failures: a start date after the appointment; a certificate that names the main applicant only; travel cover submitted where private health insurance was expected; a policy conditional on residence in a country you are leaving; and a document that is a quotation rather than confirmation of cover in force.

If you are still assembling the file, the sequence that works is to confirm your consulate's or AIMA's checklist first, then arrange cover whose certificate matches it. Frequently asked questions

Does a Portuguese health policy qualify for a residence visa, or do I need international cover?
Both are accepted on the residence routes. A Portuguese policy is often the cheaper and perfectly adequate answer; an international policy tends to produce a clearer English-language certificate and covers you before you have a Portuguese address or NIF. The decision should follow the checklist your consulate or AIMA applies to your file, not a general rule.
Is travel insurance enough for a D7 or D8 application?
It can be enough for the short entry stage, but a travel policy is written for trips away from your home country and is often questioned once Portugal becomes your habitual residence. For a residence route, private health insurance is generally the more robust document, and it is what most caseworkers are looking for.
Can I get insured before I have a NIF or a Portuguese address?
Usually yes. International policies are typically issued on your passport details and current address, which is what makes them practical at the visa stage. Some Portuguese insurers will also issue before you are resident. Tell the intermediary at the outset which documents you do not yet have so the route is chosen around that.
Do I still need private insurance once I have my residence permit and SNS access?
Not as a legal matter, although proof of cover may be requested again at renewal. Most residents keep private cover for speed of access to consultations and diagnostics, choice of private hospital and service in English. Private insurance sits alongside the SNS rather than replacing it.
Does everyone in the family need to be named on the certificate?
Yes. Every applicant and dependant in the file should appear by name on the insurance document, with dates of birth where the insurer includes them. A certificate that names only the main applicant is one of the most common reasons a family file is sent back for correction.
