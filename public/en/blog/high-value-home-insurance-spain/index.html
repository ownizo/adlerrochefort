Insuring a High-Value Home in Spain: the Standard to Expect, Region by Region

Above a certain rebuild cost, a standard Spanish hogar policy stops being the right instrument. This is the standard a high-value home in Spain should be insured to — and what changes from one region to the next.

August 2026

The standard to expect

The high-net-worth wordings available in Spain are built differently from the mass-market seguro de hogar. Before recommending one, these are the conditions we look for in writing:

  • On-site appraisal and rebuild cost. For higher-value homes the insurer carries out an on-site appraisal at no cost to you, to confirm the rebuild cost, advise on contents and valuables sums insured and recommend risk-management measures.
  • No proportional reduction. Once the recommended sums are accepted, the insurer waives average: a partial loss is paid in full, even if building costs have moved since.
  • Rebuild guaranteed. After a total loss the house is rebuilt even if the cost exceeds the buildings sum insured — provided the appraisal's recommended sums were accepted.
  • Comparable accommodation. Alternative accommodation to a comparable standard, including for pets and horses, for as long as the house cannot be lived in — not the few months typical of the retail market.
  • Grounds, walls and outbuildings. Trees, shrubs and lawns, boundary and retaining walls, pools, annexes and guest houses carried at their own sums insured — not capped at a token figure.
  • Water, gas and trace and access. Finding and repairing escapes of water, gas or heating oil with no separate sub-limit, and the water or oil that is lost.
  • Settlement your way. A cash settlement, or repair by the suppliers, craftsmen and restorers you choose, with no penalty either way.

Rebuild cost and the proportional rule

The rebuild cost of an architect-designed villa, a finca with outbuildings or a listed townhouse is not its market value scaled up, and it is the figure most likely to be quietly out of date on an existing policy. Under a standard wording, an under-insured building is paid in proportion — the regla proporcional — even on a partial loss.

Specialist insurers answer this with an on-site appraisal, at no cost to the owner, that sets the rebuild cost. Once the recommended sums are accepted, average is waived and, after a total loss, the house is rebuilt even if the cost exceeds the sum insured. That single condition is worth more than any difference in premium.

Contents and valuables

Contents at this level are insured against all risks, worldwide, without the sub-limits for accidental damage, theft from outbuildings or garden furniture that hollow out standard cover. Art, jewellery, watches and wine are scheduled separately:

  • Agreed value. Art, jewellery, watches and collections scheduled at a value fixed at inception against a valuation — the figure paid after a total loss, with no argument about depreciation.
  • No excess. Valuables insured on an agreed or declared value carry no excess.
  • Loss in value after restoration. Where a piece is restored but loses market value, the difference is paid — and repairs carry no cost ceiling.
  • Protection against under-valuation. If a professionally valued piece proves worth more than its sum insured on the day of the loss, the policy pays beyond the agreed value within a set margin.

See also insuring art in Portugal and Spain.

Family liability and family protection

Staff, guests, a pool, a boat, children studying abroad: owner and family liability scale with the household. The wordings we place carry worldwide limits in the millions, with defence costs paid on top of the limit. The same policies extend to family protection — kidnap and extortion expenses, carjacking, home invasion, threats and stalking, cyberbullying — with professional support for the family afterwards.

Security and empty months

Insurers underwriting a high-value home expect security to match it: an alarm graded and monitored, safes rated for what they hold, and a clear account of who holds keys and codes. A house that stands empty for part of the year is normal in Spain and insurable, but the pattern has to be declared, and the conditions attached to unoccupancy — inspections, alarm monitoring, water shut-off — are part of the contract rather than an afterthought. Region by region

Madrid and Barcelona

Primary residences, often an apartment inside a comunidad de propietarios or a townhouse in Salamanca, Chamberí, Sarrià or Pedralbes. The question is where the community policy ends and yours begins: interior finishes, installations and improvements are usually yours to insure, and they are frequently the most expensive part of the rebuild. Collections tend to live here, not on the coast.

Mallorca and the Balearics

International households on the islands are often second-home owners, and the house, its seasonal occupancy and, where relevant, a boat kept locally belong in the same review rather than three separate conversations. Stone fincas and cliff-top villas raise rebuild-cost and access questions; a house let for part of the summer has to be declared as such.

Costa del Sol

From Marbella and Benahavís to Sotogrande: villas with pools, outbuildings and staff, contents that reflect years of collecting, and cars left in Spain between visits. The concentration of high-value homes means the same gaps appear repeatedly — contents sums set at purchase, walls and gardens capped at a token figure, and liability limits nobody has checked.

Costa Blanca

Jávea, Moraira and Altea: hillside villas with retaining walls and terraced gardens, where storm water and slope matter as much as the house itself, and where many homes stand empty for part of the year.

The Canary Islands

Year-round occupancy and a different climate: wind, salt and volcanic terrain shape the building risk, and distance from the mainland shapes how quickly repairs can be made. Alternative accommodation to a comparable standard matters more here than almost anywhere.

Claims at this level

At this end of the market, how a claim is handled matters as much as what is covered: whether the owner can choose between a cash settlement and repair by their own craftsmen and restorers, whether the excess is waived on a large loss, and who deals with the loss adjuster. We handle the claim for the policies we place, in writing, until it is settled.

Where this leads

A high-value home is usually one part of a wider household — other homes, collections, cars, health. See private client insurance in Spain.

Frequently asked questions

Is a high-value home harder to insure in Spain?

Not harder, but differently. Specialist insurers look closely at rebuild cost, security and occupancy, and in return offer conditions a standard policy does not: no proportional reduction once the appraisal sums are accepted, a guaranteed rebuild after a total loss, and agreed values on scheduled items.

Do you insure fine art, jewellery and collections in Spain?

Yes. Scheduled items are insured on an agreed value, worldwide, with no excess on scheduled valuables. Items above a stated threshold need a recent valuation, and we tell you exactly what the underwriter will ask for.

Can a home in Spain share a policy with a home in Portugal?

Often, where the insurer writes both locations: several residences, contents and valuables under one policy with one renewal date. Where it is not possible, we align the policies so no gap opens between them.

Should a high-value property be reviewed alongside my other insurance?

Yes. A property at this level is usually one part of a wider household picture — other homes, vehicles, collections, health — and gaps in one policy are often hidden by the date or limit on another.
