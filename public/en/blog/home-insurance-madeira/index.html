Insuring a high-value home in Madeira: quintas, slopes and the island perils

Published 24 September 2026 · 8 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

Part of our guide to high-value home insurance in Portugal. For the household as a whole — collections, family liability, family protection — see private clients.

Madeira has become one of the most sought-after addresses in Portugal for international owners: restored quintas above Funchal, contemporary villas on the slopes of Calheta and Ponta do Sol, penthouses on the Funchal waterfront, and second homes on Porto Santo. The houses are Portuguese risks, insured under Portuguese law — but the island asks questions of a policy that a mainland wording rarely has to answer.

This guide sets out what those questions are, and the conditions a high-value home on the island should be insured on.

The island perils, stated plainly

Madeira’s south coast is built at the foot of steep volcanic catchments. When rain is intense, the ribeiras do not rise like a mainland river: they carry water, rock and soil down to the towns at their mouths. The reference event is the aluvião of 20 February 2010, a flash flood and debris flow that hit Funchal and Ribeira Brava hardest — the most serious of its kind on the island in two centuries.

For insurance, the point is the wording. Cover for rainwater entering a building is not automatically cover for a debris flow, the soil that comes with it, or the retaining wall that fails because of it. Water from the sea is a separate line again, often excluded or limited even where rain flood is covered; landslip, where offered, is elective and carries its own excess. On a high-value home each of these is read in the wording before a policy is recommended — never inferred.

Seismic hazard in the archipelago is lower than on the mainland and in the Azores, but not absent: on 7 March 2020 a magnitude 5.2 earthquake south of Funchal was felt across Madeira and Porto Santo. Earthquake cover is an optional extension with its own excess, and on a house built into a slope it is a decision to take deliberately.

Slopes, walls and terraces are part of the house

A Madeira quinta is rarely just a building. It is a house on terraces held up by retaining walls, with access lanes, water tanks, gardens, a pool cut into the hillside and often a guest house below. On a retail policy these are capped at a token figure, or left out. On the private-client wordings we place, walls, grounds and outbuildings carry their own sums insured, and retaining walls are not reduced to a sub-limit.

What an underwriter needs is where the house sits relative to a watercourse and to the slope, how the terraces are drained and maintained, and how the property is reached — not the word “Madeira” on a proposal. Porto Santo is a different island: lower, sandier and without the same catchments, with wind and sea the dominant exposures. The proposal names the island.

The conditions a high-value home should carry

On an island, the consequences of a loss last longer: specialist trades and materials come from the mainland, and a house can be out of use for many months. That makes some conditions matter more here than anywhere else in Portugal:

  • On-site appraisal and rebuild cost. For higher-value homes the insurer carries out an on-site appraisal at no cost to you, to confirm the rebuild cost, advise on contents and valuables sums insured and recommend risk-management measures.
  • No proportional reduction. Once the recommended sums are accepted, the insurer waives average: a partial loss is paid in full, even if building costs have moved since.
  • Rebuild guaranteed. After a total loss the house is rebuilt even if the cost exceeds the buildings sum insured — provided the appraisal's recommended sums were accepted.
  • Comparable accommodation. Alternative accommodation to a comparable standard, including for pets and horses, for as long as the house cannot be lived in — not the few months typical of the retail market.
  • Grounds, walls and outbuildings. Trees, shrubs and lawns, boundary and retaining walls, pools, annexes and guest houses carried at their own sums insured — not capped at a token figure.
  • Settlement your way. A cash settlement, or repair by the suppliers, craftsmen and restorers you choose, with no penalty either way.

For higher-value homes the insurer’s appraisal is carried out on site, on the island; accepting its recommended sums is what removes the proportional rule and secures the rebuild guarantee.

Contents, art and Madeira wine

Homes on the island often hold what the owner values most: art, furniture brought from elsewhere, and in many cases a collection of old Madeira wine, some of it older than the house. Contents on the wordings we place are insured against all risks, worldwide, without the sub-limits that hollow out standard cover. Art, jewellery and wine are scheduled on an agreed value, with no excess on scheduled items, and with loss in value after restoration addressed in the wording. A wine collection needs its storage conditions — temperature, humidity, security — described at the outset.

Empty months, short lets and the Funchal apartment

Much international ownership in Madeira is seasonal. Retail wordings restrict theft and escape-of-water cover once a property has been empty beyond a stated number of consecutive days; private-client wordings deal with unoccupancy through agreed conditions — a key-holder, periodic visits, water shut-off — written into the contract. If the house is let, including as alojamento local, that use has to be on the policy. See second homes that sit empty for months.

In Funchal, most apartments sit in propriedade horizontal. The condominium policy insures the shared structure; it does not insure interior finishes, contents, or liability to the apartment below — which on a high-value penthouse are usually the largest part of the rebuild. See where the condominium policy ends and yours begins.

Family liability on the island

Guests, staff, a pool on a terrace, a boat in the marina at Funchal or Calheta: the liability that comes with a Madeira home is the household’s, not the building’s. The wordings we place carry family liability at limits of several million euros, worldwide, with defence costs paid on top of the limit — covering the household in Madeira, on the mainland and abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Do you insure homes in Madeira from the mainland?
Yes. Madeira homes are Portuguese risks, placed under Portuguese law. We work from Lisbon and Lagos, in writing; for higher-value homes the insurer carries out its appraisal on site, on the island.
Does a normal flood clause cover a repeat of February 2010?
Not by assumption. The 2010 event was an aluvião — a flash flood and debris flow. Rainwater flood, debris, landslip, retaining-wall failure and water from the sea can be different lines in a wording, and each is confirmed before a policy is recommended.
Are retaining walls and terraces covered?
On the private-client wordings we place, walls, terraces, grounds and outbuildings carry their own sums insured, and retaining walls are not reduced to a sub-limit. On a retail policy they are often capped at a token figure or excluded — which on a Madeira quinta can be the largest part of the loss.
Is Porto Santo the same risk as Funchal?
No. Porto Santo is lower and sandier, without the same steep catchments; wind and sea dominate. The proposal names the island.
Is earthquake included?
Only where the policy shows it, with a sum against it. Seismic hazard in the archipelago is lower than on the mainland, not absent — a magnitude 5.2 south of Funchal was felt across both islands in March 2020. The extension is optional and has its own excess.
The house is empty for part of the year, or let. Does that matter?
Yes. Unoccupancy and letting both have to be declared. Private-client wordings handle empty months through agreed conditions — key-holder, visits, water shut-off — written into the contract, rather than a clause that quietly suspends cover.
