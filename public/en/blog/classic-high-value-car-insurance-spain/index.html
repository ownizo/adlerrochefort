Collector Cars · Spain

Classic and high-value car insurance in Spain: historic status, agreed value and collections

Published 11 October 2026 · 6 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

Spain has a deep car culture and a climate that is kind to old metal. From the hill roads above Ronda to the coast road of the Costa Brava, from the concours lawns of Madrid to the Mallorca classic rallies, it is one of the best countries in Europe to own and use a classic — and, on the Costa del Sol in particular, one of the places with the highest concentration of modern supercars.

Both kinds of car share a problem: an ordinary motor policy is written for ordinary cars. It pays the car’s market value after a total loss, it assumes everyday use, and it knows nothing about provenance, originality or the cost of a specialist restorer. This article covers the Spanish rules for historic vehicles, how agreed value works, and what a collection or a high-value car in Spain actually needs. Our companion pieces cover classic cars in Portugal and luxury cars in Portugal and Spain.

Car registered in Spain? For classic cars on Spanish plates we place Hiscox Vehículos Clásicos — see classic car insurance in Spain. The policies described below are placed in Portugal and require Portuguese registration.

How we insure classic and luxury cars: two separate policies

A specialist car policy in Portugal covers damage to the car itself. It never includes third-party liability, which is compulsory. Every car we insure therefore carries two policies, and we arrange both.

1. Third-party liability (compulsory)

Arranged separately for each vehicle as the base motor policy, with a limit of up to €50 million. It is the cover that lets the car on the road; the specialist policy then protects its value.

2a. Classic and collector cars

Collector Car Insurance from Lloyd's Insurance Company S.A., placed through Innovarisk.

  • Loss of or physical damage to the car up to the sum insured, at the garaging address and on the road within the territories in the schedule.
  • Agreed value: after a total loss the insurer pays the value stated in the schedule; a partial loss is paid at the reasonable cost of repair.
  • No proportional rule (average) applied to a claim.
  • Several cars on one policy, each with its own agreed value.
  • If a car is recovered after a total-loss settlement, you can buy it back within 60 days.

Conditions

  • Portuguese registration required. The car does not need to be classified as a historic vehicle.
  • Up to 3,000 km per year per vehicle.
  • Driven only by the drivers named in the schedule.
  • Excluded: racing, track days, speed tests, rallies and off-road use; hire or commercial use; wear, rust, mechanical breakdown and depreciation.

2b. Modern high-value cars

Luxury Car Insurance from Liberty Mutual Insurance Europe (Liberty Specialty Markets), placed through Innovarisk.

  • Loss of or physical damage to the car up to the sum insured, at the risk address and on the road within the territories in the schedule.
  • Total loss settled on agreed value or market value, as set in the schedule; partial losses at the reasonable cost of repair, with repairs approved in advance by the insurer.
  • Theft: the car is treated as stolen if it is not recovered within 60 days.
  • Replacement of locks after the loss or theft of keys, ignition card or remote, with a €1,000 excess.
  • Proportional rule waived; after a total loss, the settlement takes account of the amount needed to repay any finance on the car.

Conditions

  • Only available together with a Liberty home insurance policy, which for a permanent residence requires contents of at least €150,000.
  • Named drivers and an annual mileage limit stated in the schedule.
  • Excluded: racing, circuits, speed tests and off-road use; hire or commercial use; wear, mechanical breakdown and depreciation.

What we need to quote

  • A list of the cars: make, model, year and registration.
  • The value you want insured, with a recent valuation or invoice and photographs.
  • Expected annual mileage and where each car is kept overnight.
  • For each driver: full name, Portuguese tax number (NIF), full address, date of birth and the date the licence was issued.

Both specialist policies are Portuguese contracts. For a car registered in Spain, send us the details and we will tell you in writing what can be placed. Cover is always subject to the insurer's acceptance and to the policy conditions.

Spain’s historic vehicle rules

Spain replaced its 1995 rules with a new Reglamento de Vehículos Históricos, in force since 1 October 2024. The main points for an owner:

  • Thirty years. A vehicle can qualify as historic when it was made or first registered at least thirty years ago, is no longer in production and is kept in its original condition — or with modifications accepted under the rules.
  • Two routes. Many cars keep their ordinary plate and registration document and carry a yellow circular badge with a black “H” on the windscreen. Others are registered as historic at the Jefatura de Tráfico after a technical report from a recognised technical service.
  • Less frequent ITV. Historic cars between 30 and 40 years old are inspected every two years, between 40 and 45 every three, and over 45 every four; cars over 60 years old are exempt, though they may be inspected voluntarily.
  • Clubs. Non-original modifications have to be certified by a club or body connected with historic vehicles — one more reason why club membership is part of owning a classic in Spain.

Historic status is an administrative matter, not an insurance one. But it matters to the insurer: it is evidence of originality and condition, and many classic car policies are written specifically for cars used in the way the historic rules contemplate — occasionally, with care, and not as the daily car.

Agreed value, not market value

The single most important clause in a classic or high-value car policy is the basis of settlement.

  • Market value (valor venal) is what an ordinary policy pays after a total loss. For a classic it is almost always too low, and it is decided after the loss, by the insurer.
  • Agreed value (valor convenido) is fixed at the start, usually on the basis of a recent valuation, photographs and documents. After a total loss, that is the figure paid.

Values of good classics have moved a great deal in the last fifteen years, so the agreed value needs reviewing every few years, and after any restoration. For a modern supercar, the equivalent questions are whether the policy pays new-for-old in the early years, how it treats the options and personalisation that add a large share of the price, and whether repairs are made with original parts at an approved workshop.

A collection, not a list of cars

A collector rarely has one car. A garage in La Zagaleta or a barn in the Empordà may hold a pre-war tourer, two sixties sports cars, a modern hypercar and the family’s daily SUV. Insuring each car separately is expensive and full of gaps. A collection policy treats the garage as a whole:

  • one policy for all the cars, with a single schedule and agreed values;
  • cars that are laid up or under restoration insured against fire, theft and damage, without paying for road cover they do not use;
  • a defined number of drivers, often the owner and named family members;
  • cover while the cars are transported on a trailer or by a specialist carrier, and while they are at a restorer;
  • attention to the building itself — the garage, its fire protection and its security are part of the risk.

How the car is used

Underwriters of fine cars ask about use as carefully as about value.

  • Mileage and purpose. Classic policies often assume limited annual mileage and leisure use. A car driven every day needs a different policy.
  • Rallies, concours and tours. Regularity rallies and club events on public roads are usually insurable; timed competition and circuit days are normally excluded and need specific cover.
  • Track days. A morning at Ascari or the Circuito de Jerez is excluded by almost every road policy. If the car goes on a track, it needs a track-day policy for that day.
  • Keyless theft. For modern high-value cars, relay theft of keyless systems is a real risk; insurers may ask about garaging, tracking devices and how the keys are kept.

Foreign plates and moving to Spain

Many classic and high-value cars in Spain arrive with their owners on foreign plates. Spanish rules require residents to register their cars in Spain within a limited period after becoming resident, with a technical inspection and the relevant taxes, and a car registered abroad but kept permanently in Spain is not a simple risk for a Spanish insurer. For a classic, the move is often the moment to look at historic status at the same time as registration.

Until the car is registered in Spain, the cover question has to be answered carefully: what the foreign policy allows, for how long, and what happens after a claim. We look at that before the car crosses the border, not after.

A short checklist

  • Agreed value for each car, based on a valuation that is less than three years old.
  • Original parts and specialist repairers named in the policy, not just “approved workshops”.
  • Laid-up cars and cars at the restorer covered, not left out.
  • Rallies, tours and transport on a trailer confirmed in writing; track days insured separately.
  • Garage, security and keys described accurately to the insurer.
  • Foreign-plated cars: registration timetable and cover until then agreed in advance.

Frequently asked questions

Does a historic car in Spain need special insurance?
Compulsory third-party motor insurance applies to historic vehicles as to any car on the road. What differs is the rest of the policy: a classic car policy normally insures the car for an agreed value, assumes limited leisure use and recognises club events. An ordinary policy pays market value and assumes everyday use.
What is the difference between agreed value and market value?
Agreed value is fixed when the policy starts, on the basis of a valuation, and is paid after a total loss. Market value is decided by the insurer after the loss, by reference to the market for an ordinary car of that age, and is usually much lower for a classic.
Are track days covered?
Almost never by a road policy. Timed competition and circuit days are standard exclusions. If the car goes on a track, it needs a specific track-day policy for that event.
Can I insure several cars on one policy?
Yes. A collection policy covers all the cars on one schedule, with agreed values, named drivers and cover for cars that are laid up, in transit or at the restorer.
My car is on foreign plates. Can I insure it in Spain?
Residents are required to register their cars in Spain within a limited period. Until then, cover depends on what the foreign policy allows and for how long. We look at the timetable and the cover together before the car is moved.
