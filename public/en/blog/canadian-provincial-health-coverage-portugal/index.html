What happens to provincial health coverage when you leave Canada

Published 14 September 2026 · 7 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

There is no such thing as "Canadian health insurance" in the sense most people mean it. Health coverage in Canada is administered by each province and territory separately — Ontario's OHIP, British Columbia's MSP, Quebec's RAMQ, Alberta's AHCIP, and so on — and what happens to your coverage when you move to Portugal is a question your own province answers, not a question Canada answers as a whole.

Why "how long can I be away" has no single answer

Every provincial and territorial health plan sets its own residency requirement: broadly, you have to be physically present in the province for a minimum number of days each year, and actually resident there, to keep your coverage active. Each plan also sets its own rules on temporary absences — how long you can be out of the province, for what reasons, and what happens once you exceed that. These are provincial policy decisions, reviewed and changed by each province independently, and a rule you have heard about applying to someone who left from one province is not evidence of what applies to you leaving from another.

[VERIFY] The current residency requirement and permitted absence period for any specific province or territory are not stated here, because they are reviewed periodically and differ from one plan to the next. Confirm the current position directly with your own provincial or territorial health ministry before you leave — not from what a friend who left from a different province experienced.

What actually happens once coverage lapses

If your absence exceeds what your province allows, or your province determines you are no longer resident there, provincial coverage stops responding to healthcare costs incurred outside Canada — and, depending on the province, it can also affect your ability to re-enrol immediately if you return, sometimes with a waiting period before coverage resumes. None of that is unique to moving to Portugal specifically; it is the same mechanism that applies to a Canadian moving anywhere outside the country for an extended period.

What this means for cover in Portugal

Whatever your provincial plan does or does not cover once you have left, it was never going to be the thing that pays a Portuguese hospital or clinic directly — even provinces with a relatively generous absence allowance are reimbursing at Canadian domestic rates after the fact, if at all, not settling directly with a foreign provider. Private health cover arranged in Portugal, or an internationally-recognised policy, is the practical route for day-to-day healthcare here regardless of what your provincial plan still technically allows. Frequently asked questions

Is there a single Canadian rule for health coverage after moving abroad?
No. Health coverage is administered provincially and territorially, not federally, and each plan sets its own residency requirement and absence rules. Check your own province, not a general Canadian rule, because there isn’t one.
Will my provincial plan pay a Portuguese hospital directly?
Generally no. Even where a province allows some coverage while temporarily abroad, it typically reimburses after the fact at Canadian domestic rates rather than settling directly with a foreign provider.
What happens if I return to Canada after living in Portugal?
This depends on your province — some require you to re-establish residency and may apply a waiting period before coverage resumes. Confirm the current position with your provincial health ministry before you leave, ideally, not after you return.
Do I need private health cover in Portugal even if my provincial plan technically still covers some costs?
In practice, yes, for day-to-day healthcare — provincial reimbursement after the fact is not a substitute for cover that actually responds to a Portuguese provider directly.
