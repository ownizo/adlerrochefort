Luxury condos and resort homes: where the building policy ends

On a managed development the estate looks fully insured. It is not. The association or the resort covers the shared fabric. You cover the interior, the contents, any art, and the way the home is actually used — lived in, let, empty or mixed. We place the same lines in Portugal and in Spain.

Request a review

What the condominium or the resort actually insures

A Portuguese condominium in horizontal property must carry fire cover on the common structure. A golf or marina resort adds grounds, gates, clubhouses and the operator’s own liability. That cover is real, and it stops at the door of your unit. Upgraded kitchens, built-in furniture you paid for, contents, jewellery and your personal liability sit outside it.

The longer note on resorts is golf resort properties. The contents gap is what the condominium policy does not cover.

Mixed use is the claim that fails

Many of these homes are not one thing. Six weeks as a family house, three months on a rental programme, the rest empty. A policy written as a permanent residence can refuse a letting claim. A holiday-home wording can impose unoccupancy conditions you have never read — alarm, inspections, a maximum number of empty days.

The review is occupancy first, premium second. If a management company lets the home, ask what their programme actually insures in writing. It rarely replaces your own policy.

Spain is the same list, not a smaller one

Health, home, landlord and mixed-use, motor, life and private clients are the lines we place in Portugal. We place them in Spain too. What changes is the law, the wording and the claim path — not whether a luxury apartment “counts”. A home in Quinta do Lago and a home in Sotogrande are reviewed the same way: how is it used, and what does the building policy already buy?

See home insurance in Spain and landlord insurance in Spain.

Not the insurer the developer recommended

Developers and managing agents often point owners to one insurer. That is convenient for them. It is not a comparison. Adler & Rochefort is independent — we place with markets such as Zurich, Allianz and Hiscox when the risk fits, and we do not have to force a collection or a mixed-use home onto a wording that was never built for it.

Frequently asked questions

If my apartment is on a golf resort, am I already insured?

Only for the shared structure and the resort’s own facilities and liability. The inside of your unit, your contents, your improvements and your personal liability are yours to insure. That is the usual gap, in Portugal and in Spain.

Does letting the home for part of the year change the policy?

Yes. A wording for a permanent home can refuse a claim connected to paying guests. A holiday-home wording can limit cover after a set number of empty days. Tell the insurer the real pattern — lived in, let, empty or mixed — before a claim, not after.

Can the same broker place this in Spain and in Portugal?

Yes. The product lines are the same. The law, the wording and the occupancy rules are not. One review can cover a home in each country, including art inside either of them.
