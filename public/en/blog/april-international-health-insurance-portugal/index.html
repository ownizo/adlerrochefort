What APRIL is

APRIL is an international insurance group with decades of experience designing cover for people who live outside their home country. Rather than a domestic Portuguese product translated into English, APRIL's plans are built from the ground up for an internationally mobile audience — with English and French documentation, simplified medical underwriting and a reimbursement model that gives you freedom to choose your own doctors.

The advantages: why expats choose APRIL

  • Service in English and French — policy wording, customer service and claims are handled in your language, which removes one of the biggest friction points expats hit with domestic insurers.
  • Portability — cover is designed to travel with you, which suits people who split time between countries or move again within a few years.
  • Simplified onboarding — acceptance criteria and documentation are lighter than a typical Portuguese insurer, so you can often start without a long Portuguese paper trail.
  • Reimbursement freedom — you can use any doctor or hospital, pay and claim back, rather than being limited to a fixed network.
  • Strong fit for families and travellers — international families and frequent travellers value the cross-border flexibility.

How APRIL plans work

APRIL's plans are built mainly around the reimbursement model: you choose your provider, pay up front and claim back a percentage up to your annual limit. Plans are tiered, so you can scale the annual limit, the reimbursement percentage and add-ons such as dental and optical to your budget. The trade-off to weigh is that APRIL's direct network in Portugal is smaller than a domestic insurer's, so you rely more on reimbursement than on low fixed copayments.

Where APRIL falls short

A balanced review has to name the trade-offs. APRIL is not the right answer for everyone, and these are the limits we flag most often to clients:

  • Smaller direct network in Portugal — you rely more on paying and claiming back than on low fixed copayments, which some people find less convenient than a domestic insurer.
  • Reimbursement means upfront cost — you have to front the bill and wait to be reimbursed, so cash flow matters, especially for larger treatments.
  • Entry-tier annual limits can be modest — lower tiers may not be enough for a major hospitalisation, so the limit is worth checking against your real risk.
  • Less local brand recognition — some Portuguese providers are more familiar with domestic names like Médis, which can add friction at reception.
  • Price is not always the lowest — for a purely domestic, network-first profile, a Portuguese insurer can work out cheaper per year.

Who APRIL is ideal for

APRIL tends to be the strongest choice for expats who have recently arrived, internationally mobile professionals, retirees who moved to Portugal and anyone who places a high value on English-language service and freedom of provider choice. If your priority is the lowest possible copayment at a vast national network of clinics, a domestic insurer may suit you better — which is exactly the kind of trade-off an intermediary can map for you.

The alternatives: Allianz and Médis

No single insurer wins for everyone, so APRIL should always be weighed against the two other names expats shortlist. In short: APRIL leads on expat onboarding and English/French service; Allianz is strongest on premium international plans and reimbursement; Médis offers the largest in-Portugal network and low copayments. For a side-by-side breakdown of premiums, waiting periods and networks, read our independent Allianz vs APRIL vs Médis comparison, or see our general guide to health insurance for expats in Portugal.

The verdict: is APRIL worth it?

For internationally mobile expats, recent arrivals and families who value English-language service and freedom of provider choice, APRIL is often well worth it — the onboarding and portability genuinely solve problems domestic insurers do not. If your life is settled in Portugal and your priority is the lowest copayment at a vast local network, one of the alternatives above will likely serve you better. The honest answer depends on your profile, which is exactly what a comparison should settle.
