How to claim for water damage on a Portuguese home policy

Water damage — danos por água — is the single most frequent claim on Portuguese home policies, and the one most often paid at less than the owner expected. It is rarely because the insurer is acting in bad faith. It is because the cover is narrower than people assume, the sub-limits are lower than people notice, and the first 48 hours are usually handled in a way that makes the claim harder to prove.

Here is how to handle one properly.

The first 48 hours

  1. Stop the water. Close the mains stopcock. This is also a policy duty — you are contractually required to take reasonable steps to limit the loss, and failing to do so is a legitimate ground for reducing a claim.
  2. Photograph everything before you touch it. Wide shots showing the room, close shots showing the damage, the water on the floor, the stain on the ceiling, the affected contents. Timestamped phone photos are perfectly good evidence. This is the step people skip and later regret.
  3. Call the policy’s 24-hour assistance line, not your own plumber. Almost every Portuguese home policy includes emergency assistance that will dispatch a plumber at the insurer’s cost. Using it creates a contemporaneous record of the incident. Calling your own plumber first is not fatal, but you must keep the invoice and the plumber’s written description of the cause.
  4. Report the claim (participação de sinistro) promptly. Most wordings require notification within eight days, and some within less. Do it even if you do not yet know the extent — the report can be supplemented later.
  5. Do not start permanent repairs. Emergency work to stop the damage spreading is expected. Replastering, retiling and repainting before the adjuster has seen the damage removes the evidence and gives the insurer a reason to dispute the amount.
  6. Keep the damaged items. The sodden rug, the ruined skirting, the failed flexible hose. The hose in particular — it frequently determines whether the cause was sudden failure (covered) or corrosion over years (often not).

What the policy actually covers

A Portuguese water damage section typically responds to sudden and accidental escape of water from the plumbing, heating, air-conditioning condensate or sanitary installations, plus overflow from appliances and, on many wordings, ingress from a neighbouring fraction. It typically pays for:

  • The damage caused by the water — floors, walls, ceilings, decoration, fitted furniture and contents.
  • Pesquisa e reparação de fugas — the cost of locating the leak, including breaking into a wall or floor, and making good afterwards. This is a separate sub-limit and it is the one that matters.
  • Damage you cause to a neighbouring fraction, under the civil liability section rather than the property section.

And it typically does not pay for the failed component itself — the burst pipe, the split hose, the failed seal. The policy covers the consequence, not the cause. That surprises people and it is standard across the market.

The sub-limit that decides the claim

On a villa with a screed floor, underfloor heating and stone tiles, finding a leak means thermal imaging, then lifting a section of floor, then reinstating it. Two to four thousand euros before anyone repairs the actual water damage. If your pesquisa e reparação de fugas sub-limit is €1,500, the claim is effectively capped before it starts.

The premium tells you what the policy costs. The sub-limits tell you what it is worth.

Find the figure in your condições particulares today, not after a leak. On most policies it can be raised to €5,000 or more for a modest additional premium, and on a property with concealed pipework in a solid floor it is the single most worthwhile upgrade on the schedule. Why water damage claims get reduced

ReasonWhat it means in practice
Gradual damageA slow leak that has been soaking a wall for months is not a sudden accidental event. Insurers exclude it, and adjusters can date damp staining reasonably well.
Condensation and rising dampExcluded across the market. Very common in older Algarve properties and frequently misdiagnosed by owners as a leak.
Lack of maintenanceA perished flexible hose or a bathroom seal that failed years ago falls foul of the maintenance condition.
Late notificationBeyond the notification period stated in the policy, the insurer can decline or reduce, particularly where the delay made the damage worse.
UnderinsuranceThe proportional rule applies to water damage like everything else. A building insured at two thirds of its rebuild cost gets two thirds of the claim.
Undeclared useA property being let, or empty beyond the unoccupancy period, on a policy that says otherwise.
Sub-limit exhaustedNot a reduction so much as a ceiling — the claim is paid, but only up to the figure on the schedule.

When the water comes from the flat upstairs

In a horizontal-property building this is a three-way conversation between you, the neighbour and the condominium. The short version: damage originating in another fraction is the responsibility of that fraction’s owner and their liability insurance; damage originating in the common parts — a riser, the roof, a shared drain — falls to the condominium policy. Your own policy will normally still respond to your damage and then pursue recovery, which is much faster than waiting for the neighbour’s insurer.

Report it to your own insurer, in writing, and tell the condominium administrator in writing on the same day. Waiting for the neighbour to “sort it out” is how owners miss their own notification deadline.

The adjuster visit

For anything beyond a small claim, a perito will attend. They are usually pragmatic and they are not the enemy, but the visit goes better if you have the photographs, the plumber’s report, the failed component, and a written list of damaged items with approximate ages and values. Being able to answer “when did you first notice it?” with a date rather than a shrug is worth a great deal.

Frequently asked questions

Does Portuguese home insurance cover a burst pipe?
It covers the damage the escaping water causes — floors, walls, decoration, fitted furniture and contents — and normally the cost of finding and reinstating the leak under the pesquisa e reparação de fugas heading. It does not usually pay for the failed pipe or hose itself. The policy covers the consequence, not the component.
What is pesquisa e reparação de fugas and why does the limit matter?
It is the cover that pays to locate a concealed leak — thermal imaging, lifting a floor, opening a wall — and to make good afterwards. On a villa with a screed floor or underfloor heating that work alone can cost €2,000–€4,000 before any water damage is repaired. Sub-limits commonly sit between €1,500 and €5,000, so the figure on your schedule often decides the size of the settlement.
My leak has clearly been going on for months. Will it be covered?
Probably not, or not in full. Portuguese policies cover sudden and accidental escape of water and exclude gradual damage, condensation and rising damp. Adjusters can date damp staining reasonably accurately. Report it anyway and be straight about the timeline — an honest account of a partly excluded claim gets further than a version the adjuster can disprove.
How long do I have to report a water damage claim in Portugal?
Most wordings require notification within eight days of becoming aware of the loss, and some require less. Report it as soon as you know something has happened, even before you know the extent; the participação can be supplemented later. Late notification is a legitimate ground for reduction, especially where the delay allowed the damage to spread.
Water came from the flat above mine. Whose insurance pays?
Damage originating in another fraction is that owner’s responsibility and their liability cover; damage originating in the common parts — a riser, the roof, a shared drain — falls to the condominium policy. In practice, report it to your own insurer straight away and let them pursue recovery. Waiting for the neighbour is how owners miss their own notification deadline.
Should I use my own plumber or the policy assistance line?
Use the policy’s 24-hour assistance line first. The callout is at the insurer’s cost and it creates a contemporaneous record of the incident, which matters later. If you do use your own plumber, keep the invoice and ask for a written description of the cause — that document frequently determines whether the failure counts as sudden or as wear and tear.
