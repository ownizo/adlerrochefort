What your US driving record does (and does not) transfer

Published 13 September 2026 · 6 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

A clean US driving record feels like it should count for something when insuring a car in Portugal. Whether it actually does, and how much, is a different question — and the honest answer is that it depends on what a Portuguese insurer will accept as evidence, not on how good the record itself is.

What Portuguese insurers price on

Portuguese motor insurers typically look at the driver's age, how long the licence has been held, the vehicle, how and where it will be used, and claims history. Claims history within Portugal, and often within the EU/EEA more broadly, tends to move relatively cleanly between insurers through established no-claims-history documentation. A US record sits outside that system, and there is no single, universal mechanism that converts "12 years, no at-fault claims, in California" into a Portuguese no-claims tier automatically.

What can still help

  • Proof of how long you have held a licence. Licence tenure is generally straightforward to document (the licence itself, or a state record) and is one of the factors Portuguese insurers do weigh.
  • A claims-history letter from your US carrier, if you can get one. Not every US insurer issues a document in a form a Portuguese insurer recognises, and even where one is provided, how much weight it carries — a full no-claims equivalent, a partial allowance, or none — varies by insurer and is not something to assume in advance.
[VERIFY] Which, if any, of the insurers Adler & Rochefort places business with give specific, named weight to a US claims-history letter or licence-tenure proof has not been confirmed for this page. This is deliberately left general rather than naming an insurer's policy we have not verified — ask us about your specific situation rather than treating this page as a guarantee either way.

What this means in practice

Gather what you can before requesting quotes: the licence itself (and how long it has been held), and a claims-history letter from your US carrier if one is obtainable, even if you are not certain it will be used. Go in expecting that a Portuguese insurer's starting point is closer to "new driver in this market" than "driver with a proven US record" — and treat any acceptance of the US history as a genuine plus, not the default.

Frequently asked questions

Does my US no-claims history transfer to a Portuguese car policy?
Not automatically, and not the way it would between insurers within Portugal or the EU/EEA more broadly. Whether a Portuguese insurer gives any weight to US claims history, and how much, is decided case by case and varies by insurer.
What should I bring to a Portuguese insurer as evidence?
Proof of how long you have held a driving licence, and a claims-history letter from your US carrier if you can obtain one. Neither guarantees a specific outcome, but both give the insurer something to consider beyond treating you as a first-time driver.
Will I be treated as a new driver?
Possibly, at least in part — licence tenure tends to count for something, but a US claims-free record is not automatically converted into a Portuguese no-claims discount. Confirm with the specific insurer rather than assuming either way.
Does this apply to a rental car too?
This page is about insuring a car you own or are registering in Portugal. Rental cover works differently and usually depends on the rental company's own terms rather than a personal motor policy.
