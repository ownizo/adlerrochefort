Hiscox occupies a different position from the other insurers in this comparison, and it is worth being blunt about it at the top: this is not a retail solution. You cannot buy it in ten minutes online, the premium will not be the lowest number on the table, and for a €200,000 apartment it would be the wrong answer. What it is, is the market that responds properly when a Portuguese home is worth more than the retail market is comfortable with, or contains things the retail market values badly.

We are an independent, ASF-registered mediator. We are not tied to this insurer, we are not paid to recommend it, and on plenty of properties we place the cover elsewhere. What follows is how the proposition actually behaves for English-speaking owners in Portugal, including the parts that are inconvenient.

What Hiscox offers on a high-value Portuguese home

The whole proposition rests on four features that mainstream Portuguese wordings either lack or ration.

All-risks cover rather than named perils. A conventional Portuguese multi-risk policy lists the events it covers — fire, storm, water damage, theft — and if the loss does not fit a listed heading, it is not covered. An all-risks wording inverts the logic: everything is covered unless it is specifically excluded. In a house full of things that break in unlisted ways — a dropped sculpture, a spilt bottle on a rug, a mirror cracked while being moved — the difference is not academic.

Agreed values. For specified items the insurer accepts a figure at inception, normally supported by a valuation, and pays that figure after a total loss without arguing depreciation or market movement. Anyone who has tried to settle a claim on a twelve-year-old watch under an indemnity basis will understand why this matters.

Art, jewellery and collections treated as their own risk. Retail Portuguese policies handle valuables with a blanket sub-limit — often 20% or 30% of the contents sum, with a low per-item cap. A collection insured that way is not really insured. Specialist wordings schedule items individually, cover them worldwide, cover them while in transit or on loan to an exhibition, and can include restoration costs and depreciation in value after a partial loss.

Gardens, grounds, pools and outbuildings included by design. On an Algarve estate the landscaping is frequently a six-figure item and the retail policy caps outdoor property at a few thousand euros. Specialist wordings bring in mature planting and its replacement, the pool structure and plant, boundary walls, gates, outdoor kitchens, guest annexes and staff accommodation as part of the insured property rather than as an afterthought.

The proportional rule — and why this is the headline

Portuguese policies almost universally apply the regra proporcional: if the sum insured is less than the true value at risk, the insurer pays the same proportion of any claim, including a partial one. Declare €700,000 on a villa that costs €1,000,000 to rebuild, suffer a €100,000 fire, and the settlement is €70,000. On a large property, where rebuild costs move with construction inflation and a valuation two years old is already wrong, this is a live risk rather than a theoretical one.

Certain high-value wordings, Hiscox’s among them, either waive the proportional rule outright or apply it only beyond a stated tolerance. That single clause can be worth more than every other difference between a specialist and a retail policy combined. It is the first thing we check, and it is the reason we sometimes recommend a specialist policy that costs more than a retail one covering nominally the same property.

Individual underwriting: what to expect

There is no instant quote. An underwriter looks at the property and prices it, which takes days rather than minutes and requires material from you.

  • Photographs of the exterior, the principal rooms and any specified items.
  • Built area by floor, construction year, construction type and finish quality.
  • A schedule of security measures actually installed — alarm and whether it is monitored, safes and their rating, CCTV, gates, shutters.
  • Valuations for anything to be insured on an agreed-value basis, generally not more than three to five years old.
  • Occupancy details — permanent, seasonal, who holds keys, whether staff are employed, whether the property is ever let.
  • Claims history, honestly stated. Specialist underwriters price around claims; they decline around undisclosed ones.

The process feels heavy compared with a retail quote. It is also the reason the cover holds together at claim stage, because the underwriter already knows what is in the house.

Beyond the building: art, jewellery and collections

The building is only half of what a Hiscox-style wording is built for. The other half is the contents that a Portuguese multirriscos section caps at a few thousand euros per item, and it is frequently the half that decides whether the specialist route is worth taking at all. The mechanics are common to the whole market and are set out in our guide to insuring valuables in Portugal; what follows is how they appear on this kind of policy.

  • Scheduled items on an agreed-value basis. Individually listed pieces are written to a figure fixed at inception against a valuation, so a total loss is settled at that figure rather than at a depreciated market value established afterwards. The single-article limit does not apply to scheduled items.
  • Unscheduled contents at a workable per-item cap. Specialist wordings set the blanket single-article limit far higher than a retail policy, so ordinary household items do not each need listing.
  • All-risks rather than named perils. Accidental loss or damage is covered unless the cause is excluded, which is what brings in the losses that dominate this class: a ring lost rather than stolen, a canvas punctured, a watch dropped. The difference is explained in the guide to the single-article limit.
  • Art after a partial loss. Better wordings pay the cost of restoration by an agreed conservator and the loss in value that survives it — the element a general contents section leaves with the owner. What happens to a painting after a claim covers this in detail.
  • Items away from the address and worldwide. Scheduled pieces can be covered wherever they are, subject to conditions on how they travel; transit and exhibition loans can be written in rather than assumed.
  • Family liability. Personal liability limits on this class of wording are set well above retail levels and follow the family worldwide, including domestic incidents, sports and, on some wordings, employment of staff at the property.

All of it depends on documentation. Scheduled items need valuations an underwriter will work from — the standard is set out in what a valuation must contain — and the schedule will carry security conditions about safe rating, alarm grade and when items must be locked away. Those conditions are part of the contract, not advice.

How Hiscox compares

It is not really competing with Zurich or Allianz — they serve the mainstream property well and at a fraction of the premium. The overlap is at the top of the retail range, roughly €700,000 to €1.2m of rebuild value, where a well-built Zurich or Allianz policy and a specialist wording are both defensible and the decision turns on the contents, the grounds and your appetite for the proportional rule. Against Liberty Mutual, the distinction is value versus difficulty: Hiscox for valuable but conventional homes, Liberty for homes that are awkward regardless of value. Frequently asked questions

Is Hiscox home insurance available to buy directly in Portugal?
Not as a retail product. High-value home cover of this kind is placed through brokers, underwritten individually, and priced after an underwriter has seen the property, the security and any valuations. There is no instant online quote, and the process typically takes a few days rather than a few minutes.
What counts as a high-value home in Portugal?
There is no fixed threshold, but the retail market starts to strain above roughly €1m of rebuild value, or wherever contents include art, jewellery or collections beyond the standard sub-limits. The shape of the risk matters more than the price of the house: agreed values, worldwide cover for valuables, extensive grounds and staff liability are specialist features, not retail options.
Does a Hiscox-style policy avoid the proportional rule?
Several high-value wordings waive the regra proporcional outright or apply it only beyond a stated tolerance, so a modest under-declaration does not proportionally cut a partial claim. It is not automatic and it is not universal — it is a specific question we put to each insurer in writing before placing the cover.
How are art and jewellery insured on a specialist policy?
Individually scheduled, usually on an agreed-value basis supported by a valuation no more than three to five years old, and typically covered worldwide rather than only at the insured address. Better wordings also cover items in transit, on loan to an exhibition, and include restoration costs plus any loss in value after a partial loss.
Are the gardens and swimming pool covered?
On a specialist wording they normally are, listed explicitly: mature planting and its replacement, the pool structure and plant, boundary walls, gates, outdoor kitchens, guest annexes and staff accommodation. On a retail Portuguese policy these are usually outside the scope or capped so low as to be notional — often the largest uninsured exposure on an Algarve estate.
Is it worth it for a €600,000 villa?
Sometimes, and often not. At that level a properly built Zurich or Allianz policy — correct rebuild figure, raised liability, outdoor property added, valuables specified — usually does the job for considerably less. We will quote both and tell you which we would take. The specialist route earns its premium when the contents or the grounds are the difficult part, not simply when the house is expensive.
Can jewellery and watches be covered away from the house and abroad?
Scheduled items are normally written on a worldwide all-risks basis, which is one of the main reasons to schedule them. Conditions apply: pieces usually have to travel in hand baggage rather than checked luggage, cover in a hotel room can be conditional on using the safe, and items left in an unattended vehicle are frequently excluded. Unscheduled valuables get only a small, time-limited extension away from the insured address.
Does the policy pay the loss in value after a painting is restored?
A specialist art section pays two separate things: the cost of restoration by an agreed conservator, and the difference between what the work was worth before the damage and what it is worth afterwards with the intervention disclosed. Together they are capped at the agreed value. A general contents section pays the repair invoice and treats the residual loss in value as uninsured, which on a signed work can be the larger figure.
What security will be required for a scheduled collection?
It scales with value: a safe of a stated European valuables rating, anchored into structure; an alarm of a stated grade, often connected to a central station and set whenever the property is empty; and a condition specifying when items must be locked away. Above certain sums an underwriter will survey the property and attach requirements to the schedule. These are contract conditions, so a claim can be declined where one was not met.
