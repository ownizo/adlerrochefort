Health Insurance Waiting Periods in Portugal, Product by Product

Published 13 September 2026 · 6 min read
Hugo Gonçalves
Founder & Risk Management Specialist · Adler & Rochefort

A período de carência — a waiting period — is a stretch at the start of a Portuguese health policy during which a specific type of care is not yet covered. The length is not uniform across the policy: it varies by what is being claimed, and knowing the shape of that variation matters more than any single headline figure.

Why waiting periods exist at all

A waiting period protects a health insurer against someone joining specifically because they already know they need a particular treatment — anti-selection. Without it, insurance for a known, imminent cost would not function as insurance. The length an insurer sets for each category of care roughly tracks how easy that category would be to anti-select against: routine care is hard to time strategically, so its waiting period is short; a planned surgery or a pregnancy is easy to time, so those periods run longer.

The general shape

Type of careTypical shape of the waiting period
Routine consultationsShortest, sometimes waived entirely on some products.
Diagnostics and outpatient testsShort to moderate.
Hospitalisation and surgeryModerate to long, and often longer again for a procedure that could plausibly be planned in advance.
MaternityLongest on most policies — commonly measured in months rather than weeks, precisely because a pregnancy is the easiest event to anti-select against.
Pre-existing conditions, membership-based route365 days on MGEN's structure specifically — a different mechanism from the categories above, applied because no medical questionnaire is used at entry.

Indicative shape, not quoted figures for any specific product — exact waiting periods are set by each insurer and vary by policy tier; confirm the actual numbers for a specific product before relying on them.

Emergency care is usually the exception

Genuine emergencies are commonly excluded from waiting periods altogether on Portuguese health policies — the waiting period restricts planned and routine care, not a genuine emergency admission. Confirm this specifically for your policy rather than assuming it, since "emergency" itself is a defined term in the wording, not a judgment call made after the fact.

Continuity from a previous policy

Coming from continuous prior cover — another Portuguese policy, or in some cases a foreign one with the right evidence — can sometimes be credited against a new policy's waiting periods, reducing or removing them. This is insurer-specific and depends on documentary proof of the prior, uninterrupted cover, so ask about it explicitly and get the answer in writing before assuming continuity will be recognised.

What this means when comparing policies

Two policies at the same premium can carry materially different waiting periods, and the shorter one is not automatically the better product once the rest of the cover is weighed — see acceptance, not price for why premium alone is the wrong first comparison. Ask for the waiting-period schedule in writing for each category of care you actually expect to use, not just the headline figure for the product as a whole.

Frequently asked questions

Are waiting periods the same for every type of care?
No. They are shortest for routine consultations and longest for maternity and pre-existing conditions, because the length roughly tracks how easily that category of care could be timed strategically by someone joining specifically to claim it.
Are emergencies subject to a waiting period?
Usually not — genuine emergencies are commonly excluded from waiting periods on Portuguese health policies. Confirm this specifically for your policy, since "emergency" is a defined term in the wording.
Can prior continuous cover reduce my waiting period?
Sometimes, with the right documentary evidence of uninterrupted prior cover. It is insurer-specific — ask explicitly and get the answer in writing rather than assuming it applies.
Is the 365-day MGEN figure the same as other waiting periods?
No — it is a specific mechanism for pre-existing conditions on a membership-based policy that accepts members without a medical questionnaire, not a general waiting period applied across all care.
