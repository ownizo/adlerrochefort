What you need to insure a property in Portugal: the document checklist

Insuring a Portuguese property is not paperwork-heavy compared with buying one, but the documents an insurer wants are specific, and having them ready is the difference between a quote today and a quote next week. Here is the complete list, what each one proves, and where to get it.

The essentials

1. Caderneta predial urbana

The tax register entry for the property, issued by the Autoridade Tributária. It gives the artigo matricial number, the location, the built area (área bruta privativa and área bruta dependente), the year of construction, the type of use and the fiscal value. Insurers use it for the built area and construction year, which together drive the rebuild calculation. Download it free from the Portal das Finanças, or ask your accountant.

2. Certidão permanente do registo predial

The land registry certificate, proving who owns the property and what charges sit on it — a mortgage, a usufruct, an easement. It establishes insurable interest, which is the legal requirement that the policyholder actually stands to lose something. If a bank holds a mortgage, the insurer needs to know. Obtainable online from predialonline.pt with an access code, valid for six months.

3. Licença de utilização

The habitation licence issued by the câmara municipal, confirming the building is legally authorised for its stated use. Not always requested at quote stage for a mainstream property, and almost always relevant after a substantial claim. Properties built before 1951 generally predate the requirement. If yours is missing or does not match what is standing, read our article on insuring a property that is not fully legalised before you do anything else.

4. Your NIF and identification

The Portuguese tax number of the policyholder, plus passport or residence card. For a company-owned property, the company’s NIPC, the certidão permanente comercial and identification of whoever signs. The policyholder must be the owner — see property held through a company structure if the ownership is not a simple individual one.

5. Portuguese bank details

An IBAN for direct debit. Most Portuguese insurers require it for annual or instalment collection, and paying by card from abroad is often not offered.

What the insurer will ask you, not the documents

A good half of what determines the policy is not on any certificate. Have answers ready for:

  • How the property is used — permanent home, second home, long-term let, Alojamento Local, or empty during works. This is the single most consequential answer you will give.
  • How many consecutive days it is typically unoccupied. See the unoccupancy clause.
  • Construction type and finish — masonry, concrete frame, stone, timber; standard or high-specification finish.
  • Security — alarm and whether it is monitored, security doors, shutters, gates, safe.
  • Outdoor features — pool, boundary walls, outbuildings, guest annexe, outdoor kitchen, mature planting.
  • Energy equipment — solar, battery, heat pumps, EV charger.
  • Claims in the last five years, honestly. Undisclosed claims are the fastest route to a voided policy.
  • Whether anyone is employed at the property.

Buying? Get these before you sign

The best moment to check insurability is before the contrato-promessa, not after the deed. The caderneta, the certidão permanente and the licença de utilização between them will tell you whether the property is straightforward to insure, and a mismatch between the licensed area and the standing building is far cheaper to negotiate as a buyer than to fix as an owner. Send them to us and we will tell you within 24 hours whether the property is insurable as it stands and roughly what it will cost.

If a document is missing

Missing paperwork is common and it is rarely fatal. A caderneta predial can be reprinted free from the Portal das Finanças in a few minutes with your credentials, and a certidão permanente is ordered online from the Predial Online service for a small fee, valid for six months. The one that genuinely holds things up is the licença de utilização, because it is issued by the câmara municipal and older properties sometimes never had one — buildings completed before 1951 are exempt, and the câmara will issue a certificate saying so.

Where a property has been extended or altered without the licence being updated, that is a real underwriting issue rather than an administrative one: an insurer can decline a claim relating to an unlicensed part of the building, and in the worst case argue the risk was misdescribed. Tell us about it up front. Some insurers accept the property as it stands with the unlicensed element excluded, which is a far better position than a policy that looks complete and fails at claim stage.

None of this needs to be resolved before you ask for a quote. We can produce indicative terms from the address, the construction year and the approximate area, and firm them up once the documents arrive.

The short version

For a mainstream Algarve property, we can usually quote from three things: the caderneta predial, an honest description of how the property is used, and your current policy if you have one. Everything else is refinement. Send those and you will have a written comparison in English within 24 hours.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum I need to get a home insurance quote in Portugal?
For a mainstream property: the caderneta predial urbana (for the built area and construction year), an accurate statement of how the property is used, and your current policy if you have one. Everything else refines the quote rather than enabling it. With those three we can normally come back within 24 hours.
Where do I get the caderneta predial?
Free from the Portal das Finanças under Serviços — Património — Consultar Património, using your NIF login. Your accountant or solicitor can also pull it. It shows the artigo matricial, the built areas, the construction year and the fiscal value, all of which the insurer uses.
Do I need a licença de utilização to insure a property?
Not always at quote stage for a mainstream property, but it becomes highly relevant after a substantial claim, when the adjuster compares what was damaged with what was licensed. Buildings from before 1951 generally predate the requirement. If the licence is missing or does not match the standing building, deal with it before you rely on the policy.
Can I insure a Portuguese property without a Portuguese bank account?
It is difficult with most Portuguese insurers, which collect by direct debit from a Portuguese IBAN. Some arrangements can be made, particularly on specialist or high-value policies. If you do not yet have an account, tell us early and we will steer the placement towards an insurer that can accommodate it.
The property is owned by a company. What extra documents are needed?
The company’s NIPC, the certidão permanente comercial showing who can bind the company, identification of the signatory, and confirmation that the company is the registered owner on the land registry. The policyholder has to be the owning entity for the insurable interest to line up, and some retail insurers will not write a corporate policyholder at all.
Should I get the documents before or after I buy?
Before, ideally before the contrato-promessa. The caderneta, the certidão permanente and the licença de utilização together tell you whether the property is straightforward to insure. A discrepancy between the licensed and the standing building is something you can negotiate as a buyer and have to pay for as an owner.
