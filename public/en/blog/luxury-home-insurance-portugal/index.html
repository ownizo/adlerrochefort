For many international residents, a home in Portugal is more than a place to live — it is a villa on the Algarve coast, a restored quinta in the Alentejo, a townhouse in Lisbon or Cascais, or a holiday property kept ready for the family throughout the year. These homes often contain the things that matter most: art, jewellery, fine furniture, wine, and the careful work of designers and landscapers. Yet they are frequently insured under a standard Portuguese multi-risk policy that was never designed for property of this value.

This guide explains how luxury home insurance works in Portugal, what "all-risks" cover actually means, and why reinforced capital and the right policy terms make the difference between a claim that is paid in full and one that leaves you out of pocket.

What does "all-risks" home insurance mean?

Most home insurance in Portugal is written on a "named perils" basis. That means the policy lists the specific events it covers — fire, storm, water damage, theft, and so on — and anything not on that list is simply not covered. If something happens that the policy did not anticipate, there is no claim to make.

An all-risks policy works the other way around. Instead of listing what is covered, it covers accidental loss or damage to your property and contents unless a cause is specifically excluded. This is a fundamentally broader form of protection, designed to cover the situations a traditional, named-perils policy typically leaves out — with protection for theft and accidental damage offered without the usual restrictions.

With named perils, you are covered only for what the policy lists. With all-risks, you are covered for everything the policy does not exclude. For a high-value home, that difference is decisive.

Why reinforced capital matters

"Reinforced capital" simply means insuring the building and its contents for sums that reflect their true value. Standard products are calibrated for mainstream homes, and their limits — particularly for valuables — are often far too low for a luxury property. A specialist solution is built for higher values from the outset, with a minimum insured capital starting at €150,000 and the capacity to scale well beyond it.

The point of reinforced capital is not simply a bigger number. It is about making sure the sum insured genuinely matches the cost of rebuilding the property and replacing its contents, so that a claim is settled in full rather than scaled down.

The proportional rule: the trap that catches the underinsured

One of the most important — and least understood — features of Portuguese home insurance is the proportional rule (regra proporcional). Under a traditional policy, if your home is insured for less than its real value, the insurer reduces any claim payment in the same proportion as the underinsurance.

In practice: if a property worth €1,000,000 is insured for only €600,000, it is 60% insured. After a €100,000 claim, the insurer may pay only €60,000 — even though the loss is well within the sum insured. The shortfall falls on the homeowner.

A key advantage of a specialist luxury solution is that it does not apply the proportional rule in the way traditional policies do. Combined with correctly reinforced capital, this removes one of the most common and painful surprises homeowners face at claim time.

  • All-risks cover: accidental loss or damage to the building and contents, without the restrictions of theft or accidental-damage cover found in traditional policies
  • Protection for valuable items: dedicated sections for art and valuables, covering transport, handling and accidental damage — including works of art and jewellery
  • Outdoor spaces: cover for gardens, swimming pools, annexes, walls, fences and belongings kept outside — the parts of a luxury property a standard policy often ignores
  • Comprehensive limits: higher cover limits suited to high-value homes, rather than the caps of a mass-market product

  • Improvements after a claim: cover for building improvements following a loss, including sustainability upgrades
A luxury home is more than walls and a roof. It is the art on those walls, the garden around it, and the pieces that make it yours. The right policy insures all of it.

Built for expatriates and holiday-home owners

This kind of cover is designed with international residents in mind, and is available in two forms depending on how you use the property:

  • For foreign residents in Portugal: a solution adapted for international clients who live in the country full-time, protecting a primary home and its contents
  • For holiday homes: a specialist option for the holiday properties of foreign owners, recognising that a second home may be unoccupied for parts of the year — a situation many standard policies treat unfavourably

Each policy is approached individually, designed to fit the client's lifestyle and the way the property is genuinely used.

Practical points for expats insuring a home in Portugal

  • Insure the rebuild cost, not the market price: the sum insured for the building should reflect the cost to reconstruct it, which can differ significantly from its purchase or market value
  • Schedule your valuables: high-value art, jewellery and watches are best individually listed and, where appropriate, supported by appraisals
  • Declare occupancy honestly: if a property is a holiday home left empty for periods, this should be reflected in the policy to avoid disputes at claim time
  • Don't overlook the grounds: pools, landscaping and boundary walls represent real value and real exposure to storm and accidental damage
  • Review the cover annually: renovations, new acquisitions and rising rebuild costs all change what your home should be insured for

Frequently asked questions

At what point does a home stop being a retail insurance risk?
There is no fixed threshold, but in practice a Portuguese retail policy starts to strain above roughly €1m of rebuild value, or wherever contents include art, jewellery or collections beyond the standard sub-limits. The tell is not the price of the house but the shape of the risk: agreed values, worldwide cover for valuables, gardens and grounds, staff liability. Those are specialist features, not options on a retail product.
What does agreed value mean and why does it matter here?
On an agreed-value basis the insurer accepts a stated figure for an item at inception, usually against a valuation, and pays that figure without arguing depreciation or market movement after a loss. For a painting, a watch collection or a bespoke interior, that removes exactly the dispute a standard indemnity settlement creates.
Do high-value insurers still apply the proportional rule?
Not always, and it is one of the main reasons to use them. Several specialist wordings waive the regra proporcional outright or within a stated tolerance, so a modest under-declaration does not proportionally cut a partial claim. It is a specific question we put to each insurer when we place high-value cover.
What will the underwriter ask for on a high-value villa?
Expect photographs inside and out, the built area and construction details, a schedule of the security measures actually installed, valuations for anything specified individually, and details of occupancy — who is there, when, and who holds keys. Underwriting is individual rather than automated, which is why it takes days rather than minutes.
Are the gardens, pool and outbuildings included?
On a specialist wording they normally can be, explicitly listed: landscaped gardens and their replanting, pool structure and plant, boundary walls, gates, outdoor kitchens, guest annexes and staff accommodation. On a retail policy they are usually outside the scope or capped so low as to be notional. That gap is often the single largest uninsured exposure on an Algarve estate.
