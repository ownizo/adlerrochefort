How to Get a NIF in Portugal as a Non-Resident

Published 23 July 2026 · Last updated 23 July 2026 · Adler & Rochefort

The NIF is usually the first piece of Portuguese bureaucracy an expat encounters, and almost nothing else can proceed without it. Whether you are buying a home, opening a bank account, signing a rental contract or applying for a residency visa, you will be asked for this number early on. The good news is that you do not need to be living in Portugal — or even to have visited — to obtain one. This guide explains what the NIF is, when you need a fiscal representative to get it, the documents involved, and the mistakes that most often cause delays.

What a NIF is and what it is used for

NIF stands for Número de Identificação Fiscal — your Portuguese tax identification number, sometimes called the número de contribuinte. It is issued by the Portuguese tax and customs authority (the Autoridade Tributária, usually shortened to Finanças) and stays with you for life. The number itself does not make you a Portuguese tax resident; it simply identifies you within the Portuguese system.

In practice you will need a NIF to do most things that matter when relocating:

  • Opening a Portuguese bank account.
  • Signing a property purchase, mortgage or long-term rental agreement.
  • Setting up utilities, mobile contracts and internet.
  • Registering with the tax authority and filing returns if you become resident.
  • Supporting a residency visa or AIMA application, where it is frequently requested.

Because so many steps depend on it, getting the NIF right and early tends to make the rest of a move far smoother. It is one of the core items we help clients arrange through our relocation services.

Who needs a fiscal representative

This is the question that causes the most confusion, and the answer depends on where you are tax resident at the time you apply — not on your nationality.

EU and EEA residents

If your tax address is in an EU or EEA country, you can generally obtain a NIF without appointing a fiscal representative. You will still need to give a valid address, but there is normally no requirement for a Portugal-based intermediary to accept responsibility on your behalf.

Residents of third countries

If your tax address is outside the EU and EEA — for example the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada or South Africa — you will typically need a fiscal representative resident in Portugal in order to register a NIF as a non-resident. The representative is the point of contact between you and Finanças. Once you obtain Portuguese residency, this requirement generally falls away. Because fiscal representation is a subject in its own right, we cover it in detail in our companion guide on fiscal representation in Portugal.

The documents you need

Requirements can vary slightly depending on how you apply and who assists you, so treat the following as the usual starting point rather than an exhaustive checklist:

  • A valid passport or, for EU/EEA citizens, a national identity card.
  • Proof of your address abroad — commonly a recent utility bill or bank statement showing your name and residential address.
  • Where someone applies on your behalf, a signed authorisation or power of attorney, and identification for the representative.

Documents in a foreign language are sometimes accepted as they are, but in other cases a translation may be requested. If you are applying through a representative or concierge service, ask in advance exactly which format each document should be in, so you are not asked to re-send items later.

How to apply from abroad

With a fiscal representative or concierge service

The most common route for people who have not yet moved is to have a representative in Portugal request the NIF for them. You provide scanned or certified copies of your documents and a signed authorisation, and the representative attends Finanças or submits the request on your behalf. This avoids the need to travel and is the usual path for third-country residents, who require a representative in any event.

In person or by proxy

If you are visiting Portugal, you can attend a Finanças office or a Citizen's Shop (Loja de Cidadão) yourself. EU/EEA residents attending in person with the right documents are often issued a number the same day. You can also grant a power of attorney to a trusted person in Portugal to attend for you.

Whichever route you choose, the underlying documents are the same; what changes is who presents them and whether a representative is legally required for your situation.

Timeframes and costs

An application made in person with complete documents is frequently dealt with quickly, sometimes on the day. A remote application depends on document checks, appointment availability and, where relevant, the representative's process, so timeframes are more variable — plan for it to take longer than a single afternoon.

On cost, there may be a fee payable to the tax authority for certain requests, and any representative or concierge service will charge for their work. Fees change over time and differ between providers, so we deliberately avoid quoting a figure here. Always confirm the current amounts before you commit, and make sure you understand whether a representation fee is a one-off or an annual charge.

Common mistakes to avoid

The single most frequent problem we see is an outdated address on the Portal das Finanças. Your registered tax address drives important obligations: it determines whether the system still treats you as a non-resident, and it affects whether a fiscal representative is required. People often obtain a NIF as a non-resident, then move to Portugal and receive their residency card, but never update their address with Finanças. The record still shows a foreign, non-resident address, which can create inconsistencies with other authorities and may leave a representation requirement in place long after it should have ended.

Other recurring issues include:

  • Using different spellings or name orders across documents, which can cause the record not to match your passport.
  • Assuming the NIF makes you a tax resident — it does not; residency is a separate status.
  • Leaving the number dormant with old contact details, so official notifications are never seen.
  • Not confirming who is responsible for updating the address after you become resident.

Most of these are avoidable with a little coordination at the outset, which is precisely where having one point of contact for the whole process helps.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a Portuguese NIF without living in Portugal?
Yes. A NIF can be requested from abroad. If your tax address is outside the EU or EEA, you will normally need a fiscal representative resident in Portugal to obtain it.
Do I need a fiscal representative to get a NIF?
It depends on where you are tax resident. EU and EEA residents can generally register a NIF without a representative, while third-country residents usually need one until they hold Portuguese residency.
How long does it take to get a NIF?
In person a NIF is often issued the same day. Remote applications through a representative depend on document checks and appointment availability, so timeframes vary — confirm the current position before you rely on a fixed date.
What documents do I need for a NIF?
Typically a valid passport or national ID and proof of your address abroad. Where a representative acts for you, a signed power of attorney or authorisation is also required.
