Motor

Motor insurance is compulsory in Portugal and the compulsory part is narrow: third-party liability, at the statutory minimum limits, for the vehicle rather than the driver. Everything an owner from another country tends to assume is included — own damage, glass, courtesy car, personal accident for the driver — is bought separately here. So is the treatment of a foreign no-claims history, which some insurers will recognise on documentary proof and others will not recognise at all.

The articles in this category start with the ordinary case: what an expatriate needs to insure a car here, what drives the cost, and what changes when the vehicle is matriculated in Portugal rather than driven on foreign plates. From there they move to the vehicles that a standard motor policy handles badly. A classic or collector car insured at market value is insured for an argument; agreed value exists precisely to avoid that. A car collection is better treated as one risk than as a stack of unrelated policies. High-value and performance cars raise questions about repair networks and parts sourcing that the premium alone does not answer.

Two exclusions come up often enough to deserve their own treatment. Track days and organised performance driving are excluded by essentially every private motor policy, and the exclusion is absolute rather than negotiable at the roadside. Carrying passengers for payment — TVDE work on Uber, Bolt or Free Now — is likewise excluded from a private policy and needs the risk declared from the outset. We also cover fleet policies, which usually become the better structure from around three vehicles.
