Landlord liability cover in Portugal

A building you own can cause harm to people who are not you: a tenant, a neighbour downstairs, a visitor on the stairs, a contractor on the roof. Liability cover is the part of a landlord policy that answers for that — and it is the part most owners never look at until it is needed.

What owner liability actually means

Liability cover responds when someone else suffers loss, damage or injury and holds you responsible for it as owner of the property. In a Portuguese multi-risk policy this is responsabilidade civil, and on a let property it is the cover that matters most, because the people who might be harmed are no longer only your own family.

It has a limit, an excess and a set of exclusions, and those three things differ substantially between insurers. Whether a particular claim is met is a question for the wording and, ultimately, for the facts. Nothing on this page determines the outcome of a real dispute, and nothing on it is legal advice.

The situations that generate claims

These are the scenarios landlords in Portugal actually encounter. They are unremarkable, which is precisely the point.

Water reaching the flat below

A pipe in your wall fails and the neighbour’s ceiling comes down. By some distance the most common liability claim in Portuguese apartments.

Something falls from the building

A tile, a section of render, a balcony rail, an air-conditioning unit. Onto a car, a pavement or a person.

A defective installation

Old wiring, a failing gas installation, a water heater that was never serviced.

Injury on the property

A broken step, an unlit stairwell, an unguarded pool. See pools and the liability nobody insures.

Fire spreading

A fire that starts in your property and damages a neighbouring one.

A contractor on site

Someone working on your property causing damage or being injured. Check what their own insurance covers before they start.

Who can bring a claim

Your tenant

  • Injury or damage caused by a defect in the building.
  • Their belongings damaged by a failure of your installations.
  • Distinct from the tenant’s own liability towards others.

Neighbours

  • Water damage from your property into theirs.
  • Damage from falling elements or fire spread.
  • In a condominium, potentially the condominium itself.

Third parties

  • Visitors, delivery drivers, contractors, passers-by.
  • Guests of the tenant, who are not parties to the lease.
  • Anyone the building can reach.

Where owner liability stops

  • It is not the tenant’s liability. If your tenant floods the neighbour by leaving a tap running, that is their responsibility and their policy, not yours. This is one good reason for a lease to require the tenant to carry cover.
  • It is not the condominium’s liability. Injuries and damage arising in the common parts belong to the building’s policy. Condominium cover is a separate contract.
  • It does not cover known neglect. A defect you were told about and did not address is a weak position, whatever the policy says.
  • It does not cover contractual disputes. Arguments about rent, deposits or the terms of the lease are not third-party liability claims.
  • It is not employer’s liability. If you employ a cleaner, gardener or caretaker directly, Portugal has its own requirements — explained here.
  • It does not cover business activity. Running a hospitality operation from the property is a different exposure. Short-term letting under the AL regime has its own requirements — see AL insurance.

Everything on this page is general guidance. Sub-limits, excesses, occupancy conditions and exclusions differ between insurers and between policies, and the wording of your own policy is what decides a claim.

How much liability cover is enough

Liability limits on Portuguese home policies are frequently set at a level that made sense when the policy was written and looks thin against a serious injury claim today. It is one of the few things in a policy that can be improved for very little money, because the premium for a higher limit is usually modest relative to the increase in protection.

  1. Find the limit. On the condições particulares, under responsabilidade civil. If you cannot find it, send us the policy.
  2. Ask what it would cover. Not a broken window. A serious injury, or a fire that damages two neighbouring properties.
  3. Check whether tenants are third parties. Some wordings treat the occupier differently from a stranger. It is worth knowing which yours does.
  4. Check the excess. Liability excesses are often separate from the property excess.
  5. Ask what a higher limit costs. Frequently much less than owners expect. We will get the figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is landlord liability insurance compulsory in Portugal?

Liability cover as such is not generally a standalone legal requirement for a residential landlord, though fire cover is required for property in a condominium regime and lenders impose their own conditions. The practical argument for liability cover is exposure, not obligation: without it, a serious third-party claim comes out of your own assets.

Is it not included in my normal home insurance?

Most multi-risk policies include a liability section, but the limit may be modest and the wording may assume the owner occupies the property. On a let property both points deserve checking, because the people who might be harmed are no longer only your own household.

My tenant flooded the neighbour. Am I liable?

Responsibility depends on the cause. Damage arising from the tenant’s own actions is generally their responsibility; damage arising from a defect in the building is generally the owner’s. Which applies is a question of fact, and it is a good reason for both parties to be insured.

What limit should I have?

There is no universally correct figure, but limits set years ago are often thin against a serious injury claim. Because higher limits usually cost relatively little, this is one of the cheapest improvements available on a landlord policy. We will price the options so you can decide.

Does liability cover legal costs?

Policies commonly include defence costs within the liability section, though the extent varies. That is different from a legal expenses policy, which is a separate product and not a routine landlord bolt-on in Portugal.

What about the condominium’s liability?

Claims arising in the common parts — the stairwell, the lift, the entrance — belong to the condominium’s own policy. Yours responds to claims arising from your fraction. In practice a single incident sometimes involves both, which is when having proper cover on your side matters.

I have a pool. Does that change anything?

It raises the exposure meaningfully, and pools, terraces and gates often need declaring specifically rather than being assumed. Insurers take different views, and an undeclared pool is an avoidable weakness in a claim.

Does this cover disputes with my tenant?

No. Liability cover answers third-party claims for loss, damage or injury. Disputes over rent, deposits or the lease are contractual matters for a lawyer, and we will say plainly what is and is not obtainable in the way of legal expenses cover.
