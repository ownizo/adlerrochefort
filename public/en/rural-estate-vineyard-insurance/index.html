Insurance for rural estates, quintas and vineyards

A rural estate is several businesses under one owner: a family residence, a working farm, often a winery, and increasingly a hospitality operation with tastings, guest houses and events. Each tends to be insured separately, at different times, by different people — and the gaps sit exactly where one policy ends and the next begins.

We review estates in Portugal and Spain as a single risk, and arrange a coordinated programme through insurers, specialist markets and co-brokerage partners who write agricultural, wine and hospitality risks.

Who it is for

Owners of estates where the house, the land and a business share the same boundary walls.

  • Portugal: quintas in the Douro and Minho, herdades in the Alentejo, estates around Comporta and Sintra, and wine or olive properties in the Algarve interior
  • Spain: fincas and bodegas in Rioja, Ribera del Duero and Priorat, cortijos in Andalusia, possessions in Mallorca, and masias in the Empordà
  • Families who bought an estate as a residence and later added wine production, olive oil, rural tourism or events
  • Owners with managers and seasonal staff on site, and owners who are abroad for much of the year

The risks, and what differs between Portugal and Spain

Wildfire is the defining exposure in the interior of both countries: long dry summers, large areas of forest and scrub, and fire seasons that have become longer. Insurers look closely at fuel management around buildings, access for fire services and water supply. Beyond fire, the typical losses are storm and hail on crops and roofs, escape of water in cellars, theft of machinery and fuel, contamination or spoilage of wine, and liability claims from visitors.

What a well-placed programme includes

Not every estate needs every section. The point is that each one exists deliberately, with values that match the estate.

The main house and guest houses

Rebuild cost for traditional and listed buildings, guest houses and staff cottages, with alternative accommodation to a comparable standard.

Outbuildings, walls and water

Barns, stables, boundary and retaining walls, boreholes, tanks, reservoirs and small dams, each at its own value rather than a token figure.

Contents, art and collections

Furniture, art and collections on an all-risks basis, with agreed values where a valuation supports them.

Wine stock and cellar

Wine in barrel and bottle on a stated valuation basis — cost of production or market value, with agreed values for fine stock — plus cellar equipment and spoilage after breakdown or temperature failure.

Machinery and vehicles

Tractors, harvesters and farm vehicles, including theft, breakdown and the liability of vehicles used on public roads.

Crops

Climatic cover for vines, olives and other crops, normally as a separate layer through the state-supported agricultural insurance system of each country.

Staff

Compulsory workers’ cover for permanent and seasonal staff, and employer’s liability above it.

Public and product liability

Liability for visitors, tastings, events and weddings, and product liability for wine and olive oil sold, including exports.

Environmental liability

Pollution from fuel, fertilisers or winery waste water, including clean-up costs.

Business interruption

Loss of income from the winery, rural tourism or events after an insured loss such as fire.

What usually goes wrong

  • A home policy on a working estate. A household policy generally excludes business activity. Once wine is sold, guests pay to stay, or events are held, the commercial side needs its own cover.
  • Wine stock at the wrong value. A cellar insured at cost of production is paid at cost of production. For aged or fine stock, the valuation basis must be agreed in advance.
  • Walls, water and land left out. Retaining walls, terraces, boreholes and reservoirs are expensive to rebuild and often capped or excluded by standard wordings.
  • Seasonal workers without cover. In Portugal every worker must be covered by workers’ accident insurance from the first day; harvest staff are a frequent gap.
  • Events on a residential wording. A wedding or a tasting for paying guests is a commercial activity. Without specific liability cover, an injury to a guest can fall between policies.

What we need from you to obtain terms

A site plan and a list of activities are the starting point. The rest can be assembled with your estate manager.

  • Location, total area, and a plan showing the main house, guest houses, outbuildings and water infrastructure
  • Construction details, age and any listed status of the buildings
  • A description of activities: wine or olive oil production, rural tourism, events, sales and exports, with approximate turnover
  • Wine stock volumes and a proposed valuation basis; machinery list
  • Staff numbers, permanent and seasonal, and existing workers’ cover
  • Fire-prevention measures, water supply for firefighting, and claims history
  • Current policies, so we can see where they overlap and where they leave gaps

The right market, stated openly

Frequently asked questions

Can the house, the winery and the tourism activity be on one policy?
Sometimes. Some insurers write residence, farm and hospitality together; more often the estate is best served by two or three coordinated policies. What matters is that sums insured, definitions and renewal dates are aligned so nothing falls between them.
How is wine stock valued in a claim?
According to the valuation basis in the policy. Standard wordings often pay the cost of production. For aged or fine wine, the basis should be agreed in advance — market value, or agreed values supported by records — so the settlement reflects what was actually lost.
Is wildfire cover available for estates in the interior?
Yes, but underwriters look closely at the risk: vegetation clearance around buildings, access, water supply and past fires nearby. Demonstrating good fire management improves both the terms and the chance that cover is offered at all.
Do we need insurance for seasonal harvest workers?
Yes. In Portugal workers’ accident insurance is compulsory for every worker, including seasonal staff. In Spain occupational risks are covered through Social Security, and collective agreements often require additional accident insurance. Employer’s liability above that is a separate cover.
Are weddings and events at the estate covered?
Only if the policy says so. Events for paying guests are a commercial activity and need public liability cover that names them, with limits that match the number of guests and the activities involved.
