Our team

Who is by your side

Hugo Gonçalves

Founder & Risk Management Specialist

Hugo Gonçalves founded Adler & Rochefort to give private clients in Portugal and Spain the kind of insurance advice usually reserved for a family office: one person who reads every policy the household holds, knows where each one stops, and is still there when a claim is made.

With a degree in Business Management from the University of Hertfordshire and ongoing training at the Chartered Insurance Institute (CII), Hugo works from a technical grounding rather than a sales one. Nearly twenty years at the forefront of global tourism technology at companies such as Hotelbeds (HBX Group) gave him a working knowledge of how international clients hold assets across several countries, and of what that does to a policy written in one of them.

Adler & Rochefort holds agency agreements with insurers such as Hiscox, Allianz and Zurich, and access to specialist high-net-worth capacity in Portugal and Spain. That is what allows a high-value home, a collection or a family liability exposure to be underwritten individually rather than rated from a table — and what allows the firm to say plainly when a well-built standard policy is the better answer.

His working method is to read the policy before recommending it, and to say plainly where the cover stops. Each client's needs are analysed in detail, cover is built around what they actually own and where it is kept, and everything is done under ASF supervision.

It is cover written for homes, contents and valuables that a standard multi-risk policy was not designed to carry: rebuild values above the retail market, items worth more than a single-article limit, several residences, collections, and liability exposures that come with staff, guests and property in more than one place. It becomes relevant when any one of those applies — not at a fixed level of wealth.
No formal threshold. In practice the case for individually underwritten cover becomes clear when a home's rebuild cost passes roughly one million euros, when contents include pieces worth more than a standard single-article limit, or when the household holds property in more than one country. Below that, we will tell you if a well-built standard policy is the better answer.
