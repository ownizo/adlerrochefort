The problem is distance, not the policy

A non-resident landlord is insuring the same building against the same perils as anybody else. What is different is everything around it: nobody can open the door, the renewal notice arrives in a language you do not read, the agent who arranged the cover chose the cheapest option, and when a claim happens you are managing it from another country and another time zone.

You cannot inspect

You are relying on a tenant, an agent or a neighbour to tell you something is wrong — and to tell you promptly.

You cannot let anyone in

A loss adjuster needs access. So does the plumber. Somebody local has to be able to say yes.

You cannot read the policy

Portuguese insurers issue in Portuguese by law. The exclusions are in there, and they matter.

Deadlines still apply

Notification periods run from the event, not from the day the news reaches you in Manchester.

Renewals happen quietly

Policies renew, premiums move, terms change. Without someone reading them, nothing gets questioned.

Payment and banking

Direct debits from a Portuguese account, or an alternative arrangement that actually works from abroad.

Set this up before you need it

The difference between a claim that runs smoothly from abroad and one that does not is almost always decided months earlier, by whether these five things were arranged.

  1. A named key-holder. A manager, an agent, a trusted neighbour. Someone who can enter the property, take photographs and admit a loss adjuster.
  2. A written escalation route. Who the tenant calls at 11pm, who authorises an emergency repair, and up to what amount without asking you.
  3. A contractor you have already chosen. Rather than the first plumber your tenant finds on a Sunday.
  4. Documents held digitally. Deed, caderneta predial, policy, inventory, photographs of the property in good condition. All of it in a folder you can reach from anywhere.
  5. A single point of contact for insurance. So that a claim is one email in English, not a series of calls in a language you do not speak.

If the property is between tenants

For a non-resident owner, a vacancy is more than lost income. It is a building with nobody in it, in a country you are not in, for an indefinite period. Insurers treat unoccupied property differently, each defining unoccupancy in its own wording rather than by any national rule, and escape of water is usually the first cover to be restricted.

  • Tell us as soon as the tenant gives notice. Not when the vacancy has been running for four months.
  • Have the water shut off. Ask your manager to do it, and to confirm in writing that they did.
  • Arrange inspections. Some insurers require them during unoccupied periods, and a dated photograph each visit is the record that settles the question.
  • Consider dedicated cover for a long vacancy. See unoccupied property insurance.

Everything on this page is general guidance. Sub-limits, excesses, occupancy conditions and exclusions differ between insurers and between policies, and the wording of your own policy is what decides a claim.

Frequently asked questions

Can I insure a Portuguese property if I do not live in Portugal?

Yes. Non-resident owners insure Portuguese property routinely. The main practical requirement is a Portuguese tax number (NIF), plus proof of ownership and a payment method the insurer accepts.

Do I need a Portuguese bank account?

It makes things simpler, because most insurers prefer direct debit from a Portuguese account, but it is not universally required. Tell us your situation and we will confirm which insurers can work with what you have.

Do I need a fiscal representative?

Non-resident owners commonly need a fiscal representative in Portugal for tax correspondence, which is a separate matter from insurance but frequently arises alongside it. We arrange fiscal representation as a service and can handle both together.

Who deals with a claim when I am not in the country?

We do the Portuguese-language part: reporting the claim, dealing with the insurer and the loss adjuster, chasing the settlement. What we cannot do is open your front door, so a named local key-holder needs to be arranged in advance.

My agent already arranged a policy. Is that enough?

Sometimes, and often not. Agency-arranged cover tends to be minimal, occasionally insures the agency’s interest rather than the owner’s, and is rarely reviewed. Send us the policy and we will summarise in English what it actually does.

The policy is in Portuguese. Can you tell me what it says?

Yes, and we do it regularly. Portuguese insurers must issue policies in Portuguese; we read the particular conditions and the exclusions and give you a written summary in English before you commit to anything.

Does it cost more to insure as a non-resident?

Residence itself is not usually the price driver. What moves the premium is occupancy: a property that stands empty for long periods is a different risk from one with a tenant in it, and that is what the underwriting reflects.

What if I use the property myself for part of the year?

That is common and entirely insurable, but the mixed use must be declared. A property let for ten months and used by the family for two is neither a pure rental nor a pure second home, and the policy should say so.
