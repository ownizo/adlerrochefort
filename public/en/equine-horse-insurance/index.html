Insurance for horses, stables and equestrian property

A valuable horse is a living asset that can be lost to colic in a night, and a keeper’s liability for what a horse does is strict in both Portugal and Spain. Yet horses are often insured casually, if at all, and the stables and arenas around them sit on a home policy that was never designed for them.

We arrange cover for horses, stables and equestrian property through specialist equine markets and co-brokerage partners, for owners, breeders and riders in both countries.

Who it is for

Owners for whom horses are a serious interest, a sport or a breeding business.

  • Portugal: Lusitano breeders in the Ribatejo and Alentejo, equestrian estates around Comporta, and riders and owners in Sintra and Cascais
  • Spain: polo in Sotogrande, Pura Raza Española breeding in Andalusia, show jumping centres around Madrid and the Valencian coast, including Oliva
  • Owners of competition horses, polo strings and breeding stallions and mares
  • Families with horses kept at home, and owners of equestrian centres open to other riders

The risks, and what differs between Portugal and Spain

The losses that matter are death or humane destruction after illness or injury, theft, veterinary and surgical costs, a horse that survives but can no longer compete or breed, fire in stables and hay barns, and injury to people. The last is the one that can be ruinous, because both legal systems make the keeper of an animal liable for the damage it causes:

What a well-placed policy includes

Horse insurance is modular. The right combination depends on the horse’s value and use.

Mortality and theft

Death, humane destruction and theft, at an agreed value supported by purchase price, competition record or breeding record, and normally a pre-purchase veterinary examination.

Veterinary and surgical fees

Treatment and surgery after illness or injury, up to an annual limit, including colic surgery.

Loss of use and permanent incapacity

A percentage of the agreed value when a horse can no longer perform the use for which it is insured.

Stallion infertility

Loss of breeding capacity for stallions, and cover for mares in foal where relevant.

Transport

Horses in transit by road, sea or air, to competitions, sales or breeding centres.

Tack and equipment

Saddles, tack, horseboxes and trailers against theft and damage.

Stables, arenas and barns

Stable buildings, arenas, hay barns (a significant fire risk), and equestrian-centre property at rebuild value.

Owner’s and keeper’s liability

Liability for injury and damage caused by the horse, extended to grooms, riders, lessees and people who look after it.

What usually goes wrong

  • Relying on family liability. Some personal liability policies exclude horses, or cover them only for leisure riding. A competition horse, a breeding operation or horses ridden by others need specific cover.
  • Values that are never updated. A young horse insured at purchase price may be worth much more after a successful season; mortality cover pays the agreed value, not the current one.
  • Stables on a home policy. Stables, arenas and hay storage are agricultural or commercial risks. A home policy may exclude them or cap outbuildings at a token figure.
  • Veterinary limits that run out. Colic surgery and aftercare can exhaust a low veterinary limit in a single episode.
  • Horses ridden by others. When a horse is leased, loaned or ridden by a professional, the policy must say who is covered.

What we need from you to obtain terms

Specialist underwriters need to see each horse on paper before they will agree a value.

  • For each horse: name, age, breed, sex, passport details, use (leisure, competition, polo, breeding) and proposed value
  • Evidence of value: purchase invoice, competition results or breeding record
  • A recent veterinary examination, and any known medical history
  • Where the horses are kept, and a description of stables, arenas and hay storage
  • Who rides and handles the horses — family, grooms, professional riders, lessees
  • Claims history and any existing cover

Frequently asked questions

How is a horse’s value agreed for insurance?
On evidence: the purchase price, competition or breeding record, and usually a pre-purchase veterinary examination. The agreed value is written into the policy and is the basis for a mortality or theft claim. It should be reviewed as the horse’s career develops.
Am I liable if my horse injures someone?
In both countries the keeper’s liability is strict or close to it. In Portugal, article 502 of the Civil Code makes whoever uses an animal in their own interest liable for the damage resulting from the specific danger of that use. In Spain, article 1905 makes the possessor liable even if the horse escapes, except for force majeure or the victim’s fault. Specific liability cover is essential.
Does my family liability policy cover my horses?
Sometimes, for leisure riding, but often with exclusions. Competition, breeding, horses ridden by others and equestrian centres open to third parties normally need dedicated equine liability cover.
Are veterinary fees covered?
Yes, as an optional section with an annual limit. The limit should be high enough for colic surgery and aftercare, which can be expensive.
Can stables and arenas be insured with the horses?
They can be insured with the estate or equestrian property, at rebuild value, with fire in hay barns and stables addressed specifically. We coordinate them with the horse policy so nothing is covered twice or left out.
