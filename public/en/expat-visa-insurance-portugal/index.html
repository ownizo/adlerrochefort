Visa and residence insurance in Portugal

Health insurance for your Portuguese visa or residence permit

Private health insurance is one of the documents most Portuguese visa and residence applications ask for. Americans usually search for this as expat visa insurance Portugal; British applicants tend to ask for private medical insurance for Portugal. The practical need is the same either way: a policy certificate that is clear, current and suitable for the authority reviewing your file. Adler & Rochefort arranges cover in English and helps you avoid last-minute document problems. Once the application is dealt with, the wider question is what else you need as a resident — see Insurance for Expats in Portugal.

Reviewed by Hugo Gonçalves, Founder & Risk Management Specialist at Adler & Rochefort · Degree in Business Management (University of Hertfordshire) · Ongoing training at the Chartered Insurance Institute (CII), London.

What changes between routes is narrower than most applicants expect. The ground rules for a usable certificate are the same whichever visa you are applying for, and they are set out once below. What differs is the type of cover that is accepted, the period it has to span, and how strictly the document is read. This page covers the five routes we are asked about most.

What the authorities require

Authorities generally want evidence that you have access to healthcare and will not rely only on emergency public care during the relevant period. Depending on the route, that can mean private health insurance valid in Portugal, travel medical insurance for Schengen entry, or proof of continuing private cover for a residence appointment. Certificates should show the insured person, policy dates, insurer, territory and core medical benefits.

  • Policyholder name matching your passport or residence file.
  • Valid dates that cover the application, entry or appointment period.
  • Clear territorial scope for Portugal or the Schengen Area, depending on the process.
  • Medical benefits shown in a certificate or insurer document.

Requirements vary by consulate, appointment type and personal status, so the reliable approach is to confirm the document checklist for your own process first, then arrange a policy whose proof matches it — rather than buying cover and hoping the paperwork fits.

D7 visa — retirement and passive income

The D7 route is used by retirees and people with passive income, including many Americans and British nationals. Applicants on this route usually need cover in place before they travel, or before the consulate appointment, which makes the start date as important as the cover itself. A suitable policy can support the file and also give you private access to doctors once you arrive.

The key is not buying the cheapest certificate. You need cover that is valid for Portugal, starts at the right time, and can be explained clearly to the authority reviewing your application. Some applicants use travel insurance for an initial visa stage, but private health insurance is usually the clearer document for a residence process.

D8 visa — digital nomads and remote workers

The D8 route is used by remote workers and self-employed people whose income comes from outside Portugal. Applicants in this group are almost always arranging cover while still living somewhere else, and often already hold a plan bought in their home country or through an employer. The practical question is therefore whether that existing plan can be evidenced for Portugal in the format the file needs, or whether a Portuguese or international policy will be clearer. We review what you already hold and tell you which of the two it is before you buy anything.

Because remote workers often move again, it is also worth checking that the policy does not quietly lapse when your address or country of residence changes.

Golden Visa

Golden Visa applicants usually already have advisers, lawyers or fund managers, and what they need from an intermediary is a practical insurance document for the residence process. The route has changed over time, but applicants still need to show they can access healthcare during their stay and residence formalities.

Authorities may request proof of health insurance for the applicant and dependants, with validity in Portugal and dates that cover the relevant period. For non-EU nationals, including Americans and British citizens, private cover is usually the clearest way to evidence this. Health insurance is best treated as part of the relocation file rather than an afterthought: a weak or poorly dated certificate can delay an appointment even when the investment side is complete.

Schengen short-stay visa

Schengen visa insurance is a different product from long-term private health insurance. It is designed for short stays and visa entry, and normally needs to cover emergency medical care, hospital treatment and repatriation across the Schengen Area, with at least EUR 30,000 of cover for the required period. The certificate should show your name, dates, covered territory and benefit level.

It can help you enter Portugal, but it may not be enough for residence, D7 or AIMA needs, where authorities may request broader or longer-term proof. Many applicants genuinely need both: an entry-compliant travel policy, and a private health plan for life after arrival. If your next step is a residence route, it is worth arranging the two together so the dates join up.

AIMA appointments and residence renewals

AIMA appointments cover residence renewals, family processes and first residence formalities. This is the route where people most often ask whether the document they already have will be accepted, usually with the appointment close. The safest approach is to bring clear, current proof rather than a vague email or an expired travel certificate.

The document should show your full name, insurer, policy number or certificate reference, start and end dates, and medical cover, and should match the appointment date and your identity documents. If dependants are included, each person should be clearly named. For expats, a renewal is also a natural moment to move from temporary travel insurance to proper private health cover in Portugal.

How to prepare your insurance proof

Before you buy a policy, check the stage of your process. A short-stay visa, a consulate application, a residence appointment and a renewal can each require a different type of evidence. The safest document is usually an insurer certificate or policy schedule rather than a payment receipt. It should be easy for an official to read without needing extra explanation from you.

Americans often arrive with a US health plan and assume it can be used as proof. In practice, a domestic US plan may not clearly state cover in Portugal, may not include local private hospital access, or may be difficult to evidence in the format requested. British applicants sometimes rely on travel cover or GHIC-related assumptions, but private medical insurance is usually clearer for non-tourist residence steps after Brexit.

Timing matters. If your appointment is next month, the policy should not start after the appointment. If your family is applying together, each dependant needs to be visible on the document. If you are still waiting for a NIF, Portuguese bank account or final address, tell the intermediary at the start so the policy route can be chosen around those constraints — we do not need any of them in place before we begin.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying travel insurance when the authority expects private health insurance for residence.
  • Submitting a certificate that expires before the appointment date.
  • Using a document that does not name every applicant or dependant.
  • Choosing only on price without checking waiting periods, exclusions and hospital network.
  • Leaving the request until the final week, when corrections are harder to obtain.
