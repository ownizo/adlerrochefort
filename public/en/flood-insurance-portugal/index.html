Five kinds of water, five different answers

Owners describe all of it as “flooding”. Policies do not. A Portuguese multi-risk policy typically separates water losses into distinct covers with their own limits, their own excesses and their own exclusions, and it is entirely possible to be well covered for one and not covered at all for another.

  • Escape of water from your own installations. A burst pipe, a failed washing-machine hose, a leaking boiler. The most frequent domestic water claim by a wide margin.
  • Water from someone else’s property. The apartment above, the neighbouring house, the common riser. Usually handled through liability and water damage cover between the parties involved.
  • Storm and rain damage. Wind-driven rain, a roof lifted in a gale, water entering through storm damage to the structure.
  • External flooding. Water arriving from outside — a river, a stream, a saturated hillside, an overwhelmed drainage system, the sea. This is the cover most often written as a distinct item.
  • Groundwater and seepage. Rising damp, water finding its way through walls or floors over time. Gradual causes are commonly excluded in any policy, anywhere.

None of these is automatically covered. Whether a particular Portuguese policy responds to a particular water event depends on that insurer’s wording, on which optional covers were bought, and on the property. There is no standard national policy, and no honest broker will tell you there is. What we can do is show you, in writing, exactly which of the five your policy answers.

Where the exposure actually is in Portugal

Portugal’s rainfall arrives concentrated. Long dry summers leave ground that sheds water rather than absorbing it, and an autumn storm can put more rain on a hillside in six hours than the previous three months combined. The resulting problems are geographic and specific.

Algarve <em>ribeiras</em>

Dry watercourses that run through towns for most of the year unnoticed, and carry serious volume after heavy rain.

Coastal and clifftop property

Sea, wind and erosion in combination. See coastal properties, subsidence and erosion.

Sloping plots

Villas cut into hillsides where surface water arrives at the back of the house rather than the front.

Basements and garages

Below-ground levels are where flood water ends up, and often where the plant, the boiler and the stored belongings are.

Older urban buildings

Lisbon and Porto buildings with original drainage and shared downpipes serving several fractions.

Pools and irrigation

Failures in pool plant and irrigation systems that are neither storm nor plumbing, and may sit outside both covers.

The questions worth asking about your current policy

Most owners discover the answers during a claim. It costs nothing to find them out now, and these are the five that decide the outcome.

  1. Is external flooding actually included? It is a distinct cover in many Portuguese policies. Its absence is not obvious from the front page of a schedule.
  2. What is the excess for water? Water excesses are frequently higher than the general excess, and sometimes calculated as a percentage.
  3. Is there a sub-limit? Some policies cap water damage well below the building sum insured.
  4. Is the cause of damage covered as well as the damage? Finding and repairing the failed pipe (trace and access) is not always included alongside repairing the ceiling.
  5. What does it say about empty periods? Water cover is the first thing that changes when a property is unoccupied. See unoccupied property insurance.

What tends to be excluded, whichever insurer you use

  • Gradual causes. Damp, rot, mould and long-term seepage are wear rather than sudden events.
  • Poor maintenance. Blocked gutters, unmaintained drainage, a roof that had needed attention for years.
  • Known defects. A problem you were aware of before the policy started.
  • Some outdoor items. Garden structures, retaining walls, landscaping and pool surrounds are often limited or excluded, and worth declaring specifically.
  • Property in a known flood zone. Not universally excluded, but it can affect terms, excesses or availability. Insurers differ, which is precisely why comparing them is worthwhile.

Everything on this page is general guidance. Sub-limits, excesses, occupancy conditions and exclusions differ between insurers and between policies, and the wording of your own policy is what decides a claim.

What to do if water is already coming in

  1. Make it safe. Power off if water is near electrics. Nothing else matters more than this.
  2. Stop the source if you can. Mains stopcock for plumbing; there is less to be done about a river.
  3. Photograph everything. Before you clear up. Wide shots and close-ups, with the water still visible.
  4. Tell us immediately. Portuguese policies have notification deadlines and we would rather start the claim today than argue about the date later.
  5. Keep receipts. Emergency drying, pumping and temporary repairs are often recoverable.
  6. Do not dispose of damaged items. Not until the loss adjuster has seen them or agreed in writing that you can.

Frequently asked questions

Is flood damage covered by standard home insurance in Portugal?

Not as a given. Portuguese multi-risk policies treat different water events as different covers, and external flooding in particular is frequently a distinct item that may or may not have been included. The answer is in your schedule and wording, and we will read both.

What is the difference between water damage and flood cover?

Water damage usually refers to water escaping from installations inside or around the property — pipes, appliances, a neighbour’s bathroom. Flood generally refers to water arriving from outside, such as a river, the sea or surface water. Many policies cover the first and treat the second separately.

Can I insure a property that has flooded before?

Often, yes, though previous flooding is material information you must disclose and it may affect terms, excess or availability. Insurers take different views of the same property, which is exactly the situation where comparing the market is worth the effort.

Does my policy cover the cost of finding the leak?

Sometimes. Cover for tracing and accessing the source of a leak — lifting floors, opening walls — is separate from repairing the visible damage, and it is not present in every policy. It is one of the specific items we check.

My neighbour’s apartment flooded mine. Whose insurer pays?

Usually it runs through the neighbour’s liability and water damage cover, though you may claim on your own policy and let the insurers settle between themselves. Having your own cover means you are not dependent on your neighbour’s cooperation.

Are storm damage and flood the same thing?

No. Storm cover typically deals with wind and rain damaging the structure. Flood deals with water arriving from outside. A single autumn event can involve both, and a policy can respond to one and not the other.

Is my swimming pool or garden covered?

Frequently limited or excluded unless specifically declared. Pools, pool plant, retaining walls, landscaping and outbuildings usually need naming rather than assuming, and the same applies to the liability that comes with a pool.

What about an empty property?

Water cover is normally the first thing that changes when a property is unoccupied, and shutting off the supply is often an explicit condition. Our unoccupied property page deals with that situation specifically.
