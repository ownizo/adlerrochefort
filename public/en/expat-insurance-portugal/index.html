Why insurance in Portugal is a different problem when you have moved here

Nothing about Portuguese insurance is designed for someone who arrived last year. The policy wording is Portuguese, the assumptions behind it are Portuguese, and the questions an insurer asks — how the house is occupied, who holds the title, how long you have held a licence, whether you are tax resident — have answers that are straightforward for a Portuguese family and complicated for almost everyone else.

The result is rarely a dramatic failure. It is a quietly wrong policy: a house described as a permanent residence when it is empty for seven months, contents insured for a number nobody has revisited since the purchase, a health plan bought for a visa appointment that turns out not to cover the thing you actually needed. Those gaps surface at a claim, which is the worst possible moment to find them.

This page exists to answer one question before you spend money: what insurance do you actually need in Portugal, and in what order? Once that is clear, each product below has its own page, with its own quote form.

Moving to Portugal? Start with the insurance you actually need

There is no single checklist, because what you need depends on what you are doing here. The honest version is a short list of questions, and the answers point at different products.

  • Are you applying for a visa or a residence permit? Applications are normally expected to be supported by health cover, and what qualifies depends on the route and on what is asked for at the time. We work from what is actually being requested rather than from a general rule — see insurance for a Portuguese visa and what counts as valid proof.
  • Are you buying, or renting? Buyers need buildings cover from completion, and a mortgage normally brings a life policy with it. Tenants insure their own contents and their own liability, not the landlord’s building.
  • Are you bringing a car? An imported vehicle has to be matriculated before a Portuguese motor policy attaches to it, and your foreign claims history may or may not be recognised by the insurer you approach.
  • Will the property sit empty? Unoccupancy is the single most common reason an otherwise good home policy stops responding. Say it out loud at quotation, not at claim.
  • Are you retiring here? Health insurance is the product where age and medical history bite hardest, and the options narrow the longer it is left.
  • Are you working remotely, or still running something abroad? Income earned outside Portugal, and businesses still held elsewhere, change which liability and health arrangements make sense.
  • Will you keep a base in another country? Splitting your year is the case international health insurance exists for, and it is worth deciding before you buy a domestic plan.

The practical parts of arriving — the NIF, the bank account, a Portuguese address for correspondence — are handled on our relocation page, and fiscal representation is there for owners who are not tax resident.

If you own property in Portugal but live somewhere else

Non-resident ownership is normal here and insurers deal with it routinely, but it changes the shape of the policy. The property is empty for long stretches, nobody local is responsible for it day to day, the correspondence goes abroad, and a claim has to be handled by someone who is not standing in the building.

  • Occupancy is the material fact A house used eight weeks a year is not the risk an insurer prices as a permanent home. Describing it accurately at quotation is what keeps the policy responding later — see second home insurance and unoccupied property.
  • Letting changes the contract Rent turns a home policy into a landlord policy, and long-term tenancies and holiday letting are underwritten differently. Start at landlord insurance, or at non-resident landlord cover if you are abroad.
  • You will need a NIF A Portuguese tax number is normally needed to hold a policy on a Portuguese property, and it is the item that most often holds a purchase up. How to get one as a non-resident, and fiscal representation if you need a representative address.
  • Claims happen while you are not here Water escaping in an empty house is the classic non-resident claim. We handle the insurer’s side in Portuguese and report back to you in English.
  • The title may be held through a company Ownership through a company or a trust changes who the policyholder is, and getting that wrong is a problem at claim rather than at quotation.

The problems international clients actually bring us

The policy is in Portuguese

By law, a Portuguese insurer issues the contract in Portuguese. We go through it with you in English first, so signing is not an act of faith.

The terminology does not translate cleanly

Franquia, capital seguro, propriedade horizontal, multirriscos, tomador versus segurado. These are not exotic words, but guessing at them is how people end up with the wrong cover.

Your insurance history is somewhere else

Years of good conduct with a British, Dutch, German or American insurer do not transfer automatically. What a Portuguese insurer will accept as evidence varies, so we ask before assuming.

Foreign no-claims history on a motor policy

Some insurers recognise a foreign claims record with the right documentation, others do not. It is worth establishing at quotation, because it moves the premium.

The house is empty half the year

The unoccupancy clause is where most second-home claims are lost. It is also the easiest thing to get right in advance.

You live in more than one country

Residency, treatment abroad and where the property sits pull in different directions. This is what international health cover exists for.

Something happened while you were abroad

A claim reported late, from another timezone, in a language you do not have, is a bad combination. We report it, chase it and translate it.

You want to change insurer or broker

Moving an existing policy is normal and largely administrative, but there are timing and notice questions worth getting right.

Nobody explained the exclusions

Exclusions are not fine print — they are the actual shape of the cover. We read the relevant ones to you before you buy.

Common expat insurance questions

What insurance do expats need in Portugal?

There is no single answer, because it depends on what you are doing here. Most international residents end up with health insurance and home insurance first, then add motor cover if they drive, life cover if there is a mortgage, and landlord cover if the property is let. Someone splitting their year between two countries often needs international health cover instead of a domestic plan. The situation section above is the quickest way to see which applies to you.

Can foreigners get insurance in Portugal?

Yes. Non-nationals and non-residents hold Portuguese policies routinely. You will normally need a Portuguese tax number (NIF), and individual insurers set their own acceptance criteria, so the practical question is which insurer will write the risk rather than whether it can be written at all.

Can I get insurance before I move to Portugal?

In many cases yes, and for some products it is the sensible order — buildings cover from the day you complete on a purchase, for example. What is possible before you arrive depends on the product and the insurer. We cover the practicalities in getting insurance in Portugal before you have a NIF or residency.

Do I need Portuguese residency to take out a policy?

Not necessarily. Non-resident owners insure Portuguese property as a matter of course, and residency is a different question from tax number. Whether a specific policy is available to a non-resident is set by the insurer and the product, so it is worth asking before assuming either way.

Can I insure a property in Portugal if I live abroad?

Yes, and it is one of the most common things we arrange. What changes is the detail: how the property is occupied, who holds a key, where the correspondence goes and how a claim is handled while you are not in the country. See second home insurance or, if the property is let, non-resident landlord insurance.

Can I keep my no-claims history from another country?

Sometimes. Some Portuguese motor insurers will take a documented claims record from a foreign insurer into account, others will not, and what they accept as evidence differs. It is worth establishing at quotation rather than after the policy is issued, because it affects the premium.

Can Adler & Rochefort arrange everything in English?

Yes — English is our working language. The questions, the comparison, the explanation of the terms, renewals and claims are all handled in English. The policy itself is issued in Portuguese by the insurer, because Portuguese law requires it; what we add is that you understand exactly what it says before you sign.

Can I have more than one type of insurance with you?

Yes, and most of our international clients do. Health, home, letting, motor, life and specialist cover are handled by the same people, which is largely the point — the gaps tend to appear between products rather than inside them.

What insurance do I need when buying a property in Portugal?

Buildings cover from completion is the immediate one, and a mortgage will normally bring a life policy with it, and it is worth asking what your options are there rather than defaulting to whatever the lender offers. Contents, family liability and, depending on the property, flood and seismic cover are the usual additions. Start at home insurance.

What insurance do landlords need in Portugal?

A property that produces rent is not covered by an ordinary home policy. Landlord insurance covers the building, the owner’s liability and, depending on the arrangement, loss of rent. Long-term tenancies and holiday letting (Alojamento Local) are underwritten differently. Start at landlord insurance in Portugal.
