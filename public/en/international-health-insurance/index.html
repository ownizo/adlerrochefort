What international health insurance is

International health insurance is private medical cover for people who live outside their country of nationality. Instead of a single national hospital network, you choose a geographic area — Europe, worldwide excluding the United States, or worldwide including it — and you can be treated at recognised hospitals and doctors anywhere inside it. Planned hospital treatment is normally pre-authorised and paid directly by the insurer, and the policy stays with you when you move country again.

It is not travel insurance. Travel insurance covers emergencies on a trip and ends when you go home. An international health plan is your main health insurance for the year: hospital stays, surgery, cancer treatment, specialists, and — if you choose them — out-patient care, dental, maternity and medical evacuation.

Who it is for

If your life is spread across more than one country, a domestic policy covers only part of it. These are the people we arrange international cover for every week.

Families relocating abroad

One contract for parents and children, a cover area that matches where you live and where you travel, and continuity if the next posting is in another country.

Remote workers and digital nomads

People who live in one country for most of the year and work from several. The insurer bases the policy on your main country of residence — the place where you spend most of the year.

Retirees living abroad

Cover that does not depend on a local public system you may not yet be entitled to, with the option of treatment back home or at a specialist centre within your area of cover.

US citizens overseas

Americans living abroad are placed with Cigna Global, which accepts US citizens resident outside the United States. We price worldwide cover with and without the US so you can see what the US element really costs.

Executives and high-net-worth families

High annual limits, private rooms, second medical opinions and evacuation — arranged and serviced by one adviser who knows the file.

Students and children at school abroad

Cover that works where the child actually lives, coordinated with the parents’ plan. See health cover for children at school abroad.

How an international plan is built

Every international plan is assembled from the same five decisions. Getting them right matters more than the brand on the card.

  1. Area of cover. Europe, worldwide excluding the US, or worldwide including the US. Including the United States is the single biggest driver of price.
  2. Core level. The hospital side of the plan: in-patient and day-patient treatment, surgery, intensive care, cancer care, and the annual limit that applies.
  3. Optional covers. Out-patient consultations and diagnostics, dental and optical, medical evacuation and wellbeing, added only if you will use them.
  4. Deductible. How much you pay yourself before the insurer pays. A higher deductible lowers the premium and suits people who want protection against large bills only.
  5. Underwriting. You declare your medical history and the insurer sets your terms. Pre-existing conditions may be covered, excluded or covered with a loading. Declaring everything is what protects a future claim.

Can I get international cover where I live?

In most countries, yes. Both insurers write international cover for expatriates around the world, but every international plan has three rules worth knowing before you apply. We check all three on day one, so you never waste time on a plan you cannot buy.

1. You live outside your country of nationality

International plans are designed for expatriates. A Portuguese citizen living in Portugal, or a German citizen living in Germany, is normally not eligible. Dual nationals should tell us both nationalities.

2. Your main country of residence counts

The policy is based on where you spend most of the year, not where you are registered. If you split 160 days in one country and 205 in another, the second one is your country of residence.

3. Some countries require local insurance

A small number of countries require residents to hold cover from a locally licensed insurer — for example Brazil, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and mainland China — and US residents need US-compliant cover. Where that applies we tell you, and explain the alternatives.

Frequently asked questions

What is international health insurance?

International health insurance, or international private medical insurance (IPMI), is private medical cover for people who live outside their country of nationality. You choose an area of cover — Europe, worldwide excluding the United States, or worldwide — and can be treated at recognised hospitals and doctors anywhere inside it. The policy is your main health insurance for the year and continues if you move to another country.

How much does international health insurance cost?

There is no meaningful average. The premium depends on the ages of the people insured, the area of cover, the core level, the optional covers, the deductible and the outcome of medical underwriting. Including the United States in the area of cover is the single biggest cost driver. The only reliable figure is a quotation on your own details, which we provide free of charge for both Allianz Care and Cigna Global.

Can I buy international health insurance if I live in my own country?

Normally not. International plans are designed for expatriates, so a citizen living in their country of nationality is usually not eligible — a Portuguese national living in Portugal, for example, cannot take Cigna Global. If you hold two nationalities, tell us both: dual nationals often qualify.

Which insurer is best for US citizens living abroad?

Cigna Global. It accepts US citizens who live outside the United States, with worldwide cover excluding or including the US. Allianz Care does not accept US citizens. For Americans we also ask for the state where you live, because it matters for tax purposes.

Which country counts as my country of residence?

The country where you spend most of the year. Insurers look at where you actually live, not only where you are registered. If you spend 160 days in one country and 205 in another, the second is your main country of residence.

Are there countries where international cover is not available?

Yes. Sanctioned countries are excluded, and some countries require residents to hold cover from a locally licensed insurer — for example Brazil, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and mainland China. Each insurer also has its own list. We confirm availability for your nationality and country of residence before we quote.

Are pre-existing conditions covered?

Not automatically. International plans are individually underwritten: you declare your medical history and the insurer sets your terms, which can be cover as standard, an exclusion or a premium loading. Allianz Care and Cigna Global can reach different decisions on the same history, which is why we present both. Declaring everything is what protects a future claim.

Is international health insurance the same as travel insurance?

No. Travel insurance covers emergencies during a trip and stops when the trip ends. International health insurance is annual, comprehensive health cover for where you live, including planned treatment, specialists, cancer care and, if you choose them, out-patient, dental and maternity.

Will the policy be accepted for my visa or residence permit?

Both insurers issue a certificate of insurance in English showing the insured persons, the dates and the benefits, which is the document most consulates and immigration services ask for. Requirements differ by country and visa type, so check the checklist for your application and we will make sure the certificate matches it.

Do you charge a fee?

No. We are paid a commission by the insurer. There is no fee for the quotation, the comparison, the application or our help with claims.
